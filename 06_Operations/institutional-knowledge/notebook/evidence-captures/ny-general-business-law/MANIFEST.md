## What the raw capture establishes (quoted, not summarized)

- **§ 396-b, subdivision 1(c)** (definition): *"'Synthetic performer' means a digitally created asset created, reproduced, or modified by computer, using generative artificial intelligence or a software algorithm, that is intended to create the impression that the asset is engaging in an audiovisual and/or visual performance of a human performer who is not recognizable as any identifiable natural performer."*
- **§ 396-b, subdivision 2** — a **pre-existing** (originally enacted by chapter 1031 of the laws of 1965) dealer-advertisement disclosure misdemeanor, unrelated to AI/synthetic performers, retained unchanged by the 2025 amendment. **Important correction to the prior research pass**, which did not identify that § 396-b is a 1965 statute amended in 2025, not a wholly new 2025 enactment — the synthetic-performer provisions are new subdivisions layered onto much older dealer-advertising law.
- **§ 396-b, subdivision 3** (the actual synthetic-performer disclosure duty): *"Any person engaged in the business of dealing in any property or service who for any commercial purpose produces or creates an advertisement respecting any such property or service, in any medium or media in which such advertisement appears, shall conspicuously disclose in such advertisement that a synthetic performer is in such advertisement, where such person has actual knowledge. A violation of this subdivision shall result in a civil penalty of one thousand dollars for a first violation, and five thousand dollars for any subsequent violation."*
- **§ 396-b, subdivision 4** (expressive-work exemption, conditional): *"This section shall not apply to advertisements and promotional materials for expressive works, including but not limited to, motion pictures, television programs, streaming content, documentaries, video games, or other similar audiovisual works, provided that the use of a synthetic performer in the advertisement or promotional material is consistent with its use in the expressive work."*
- **§ 396-b, subdivision 5** (cross-reference to the sibling NY likeness statute — new finding, not identified in the prior research pass): *"Nothing in this section shall limit, reduce, or enlarge any rights any person may have under section fifty, fifty-f, or fifty-one of the civil rights law or under any other law."* — the legislature itself explicitly distinguishes this section from NY Civil Rights Law §§ 50/50-f/51 (the statute `CLAIM-LIKENESS-NY-CONSENT-REQUIREMENT-001-v1` governs), confirming the two are complementary, non-overlapping authorities, not merely presumed distinct by SI8's own prior research.
- **§ 396-b, subdivision 6** (federal Section 230 preservation — new finding): *"Nothing in this section shall be construed to limit, or to enlarge, the protections that 47 U.S.C. section 230 confers on an interactive computer service for content provided by another information content provider, as such terms are defined in 47 U.S.C. section 230."*
- **§ 396-b, subdivision 7** (audio/translation exemptions): *"(a) Audio advertisements; or (b) Where the use of artificial intelligence solely involves the language translation of a human performer."*
- **§ 396-b, subdivision 8** (platform/medium exemption, express — confirms the prior research pass's claim on this specific point): *"Nothing in this section shall apply to any medium used for advertising, including, but not limited to, newspapers, magazines, and television networks and stations, streaming services, cable television systems, billboards, and transit advertisements, by whom any advertisement or solicitation in violation of this section is published or disseminated."*
- **AI-tool-provider boundary — no express exemption found.** Unlike subdivision 8's explicit platform/medium carve-out, **no subdivision anywhere in the captured text expressly exempts AI tool providers/vendors.** The duty in subdivision 3 is scoped to a person who "produces or creates an advertisement" for commercial purposes — an AI tool provider is outside that described class as an ordinary matter of statutory scope, not because of an express carve-out. **This corrects the prior research pass's overstatement** ("explicitly does not apply to the AI tool provider") — the correct, evidence-supported framing is that AI tool providers are not described as duty-holders, not that they are expressly excluded.
- **Enactment/effective-date evidence** (from `ny-s8420a-bill-status_...html`): action-history table entry *"Dec 11, 2025 — signed chap.617"* (Chapter 617 of the Laws of 2025); sponsor memo: *"EFFECTIVE DATE: This act shall take effect 180 days after it becomes law."* December 11, 2025 + 180 days = **June 9, 2026** (independently computed from primary evidence, not merely trusted from the prior secondary-sourced research doc, which stated the same date). **Confirmed operative as of 2026-09-10** — effective date is in the past relative to today.
- Section 1 of the bill text (visible in the redline excerpt on the bill-status page) confirms: *"Section 396-b of the general business law, as added by chapter 1031 of the laws of 1965, is amended to read as follows"* — direct primary confirmation that this is an amendment to a 1965 statute, not a new 2025 enactment (see subdivision 2 note above).
