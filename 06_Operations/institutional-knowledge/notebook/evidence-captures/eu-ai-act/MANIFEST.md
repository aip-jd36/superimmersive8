## What could not be captured (retrieval failures, recorded per SOP §5 — not evidence of absence)

Not captured. The *effect* of this regulation on Article 50(2) is corroborated by Class A evidence (the Guidelines PDF footnote 50, ¶154, above), but the Omnibus's own amending-provision text was not independently read.
- **A current, consolidated (post-Omnibus) text of Article 50 or Article 2** — not available from any source reached this session. The AI Act Service Desk pages captured above are explicitly, self-reportedly **pre-Omnibus** text (see each row's Completeness column).
- **EUR-Lex's own hosted copy of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689** (the base Act) and **of the Digital Omnibus (2026/1744)** — attempted via `legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML`, `/TXT/`, `/TXT/PDF/`, `/ALL/`, `eli/reg/2024/1689/oj` (direct and via `data.europa.eu` redirect); every attempt returned the generic "Today's OJ" landing page (HTTP 200, wrong content) rather than the requested document. A GDPR control fetch (`CELEX:32016R0679`) via the identical method failed identically, confirming this is a site-wide EUR-Lex retrieval barrier as of this session, not specific to the AI Act or to this evidence-gathering effort.
- **Publications Office metadata page** (`op.europa.eu/en/publication-detail/-/publication/dc8116a1-3fe6-11ef-865a-01aa75ed71a1/language-en`) — returned HTTP 403 from an Azure WAF on direct CLI fetch. Not captured as a durable artifact. (Its metadata — CELEX `32024R1689`, "OJ L, 2024/1689, 12.7.2024" — is independently and more strongly confirmed by the Guidelines PDF's own footnote 1, so this is not a load-bearing gap.)
- **Live browser automation** (Claude in Chrome) — the browser extension was not connected in this session, so a real-browser retrieval attempt (which might succeed where headless CLI/WebFetch failed against EUR-Lex specifically) was not possible. A human-assisted capture (SOP §3, browser Print→PDF against EUR-Lex directly) was not performed in this session either.
