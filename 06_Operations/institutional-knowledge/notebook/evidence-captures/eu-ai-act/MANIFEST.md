## What could not be captured (retrieval failures, recorded per SOP §5 — not evidence of absence)

- **The Digital Omnibus regulation's own verbatim operative amending text** (cited above as `OJ:L_202601744`, publicly reported and now Class-A-confirmed via the Commission's own `href` as Regulation (EU) 2026/1744) — **still not captured**, across two separate sessions of genuine, escalating attempts. The follow-up session specifically identified the failure mode: `eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2026/1744/oj/eng` returns HTTP 202 with an AWS WAF JavaScript-challenge page (`awswaf.com` token/cookie exchange) — a real bot-detection gate requiring JS execution, not a routing bug, and not solvable by any CLI/header technique. The *substance* of what this instrument does to Article 50(2) is now corroborated by **two independent official EU institutional sources** (the Commission's Guidelines PDF, ¶ following footnote 49/50, and the European Parliament's own EPRS briefing, EPRS_BRI(2026)782651, tracing the full legislative history of the same provision to the same final date) — but neither is the enacted instrument's own operative text.
- **A current, consolidated (post-Omnibus) text of Article 50 or Article 2** — not available from any source reached across either session. The AI Act Service Desk pages captured above are explicitly, self-reportedly **pre-Omnibus** text (see each row's Completeness column).
- **EUR-Lex's own hosted copy of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689** (the base Act) and **of the Digital Omnibus (2026/1744)** — attempted via `legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML`, `/TXT/`, `/TXT/PDF/`, `/ALL/`, `eli/reg/.../oj` (direct, via `data.europa.eu` redirect, and via the content-negotiated `oj/eng` form), across two sessions; every non-challenge attempt returned the generic "Today's OJ" landing page (HTTP 200, wrong content), and the one URL pattern that behaved differently (`eli/reg/2026/1744/oj/eng`) returned an explicit AWS WAF bot challenge (HTTP 202) instead. A GDPR control fetch (`CELEX:32016R0679`) via the identical method failed identically in both sessions, confirming this is a site-wide EUR-Lex retrieval barrier, not specific to the AI Act, the Omnibus, or to this evidence-gathering effort.
- **Publications Office metadata page** (`op.europa.eu/en/publication-detail/...`) — returned HTTP 403 from an Azure WAF on direct CLI fetch in both sessions. Not captured as a durable artifact. (Its metadata for the base Act — CELEX `32024R1689`, "OJ L, 2024/1689, 12.7.2024" — is independently and more strongly confirmed by the Guidelines PDF's own footnote 1, so this is not a load-bearing gap for the base Act; no equivalent op.europa.eu lookup was attempted for the Omnibus specifically, since the WAF block was already established.)
- **EFTA/EEA-Lex factsheet** (`efta.int/eea-lex/32026r1744`) — returned HTTP 403 via WebFetch. Not captured.
- **Live browser automation** (Claude in Chrome) — the browser extension remained not connected in the follow-up session either, so a real-browser retrieval attempt (which might succeed against a JS-challenge where headless CLI/WebFetch structurally cannot) was not possible. ## Fidelity notes

- The five AI Act Service Desk `.html` files are **raw HTML as served by the official ai-act-service-desk.ec.europa.eu pages**, extracted directly with no LLM/model processing at any point — the same evidentiary strength as the existing NY Civil Rights Law capture in this notebook's own `evidence-captures/ny-civil-rights-law/` folder.
- The AI Act Service Desk explicitly documents its own source: every article/recital page's footer states "The text used in this tool is the 'Artificial Intelligence Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689), Official version of 13 June 2024'" — i.e. it self-identifies as a faithful reproduction tool, not an independent paraphrase, which is why it is classified Class A (official platform authority reproducing primary statutory text) rather than Class C/D.
- The Guidelines PDF has a genuine embedded text layer (unlike the Artlist PDFs in this notebook, which were image-only Print-to-PDF renders) — `pdftotext -layout` extraction was clean and complete.
- The EPRS briefing is classified Class A for **what it says**, but is explicitly **not treated as equivalent to the enacted instrument's text** — it is European Parliament's own research service's account of the negotiation, not the Regulation's operative articles. It is included because it independently corroborates the Commission Guidelines' Dec-2-2026 date via a completely separate institutional source and shows the full negotiating history (6mo proposal → 6mo Council → 3mo Parliament committee → final trilogue "delayed to 2 December 2026"), which is stronger corroboration than either document alone.
