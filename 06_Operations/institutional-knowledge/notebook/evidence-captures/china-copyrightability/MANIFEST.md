## Load-bearing finding — national authority explicitly declines to decide AI-output copyrightability

Quoted verbatim from `spc-ai-disputes-opinion-press-qa_20260930T150224Z_05c5d0ff.html` (SPC's own official website, its own press office, 2026-09-07):

> 需要说明的是，在《意见》起草论证过程中，对人工智能生成内容的可版权性，以及未经许可使用他人作品训练人工智能大模型的定性问题，意见分歧很大，认识还有待进一步深化。因此，《意见》对这两个问题暂不作规定。

*("It should be noted that, during the drafting and deliberation of the Opinion, views diverged sharply on the copyrightability of AI-generated content, and on the legal characterization of using others' works without permission to train large AI models — understanding of these issues still requires further deepening. Therefore, the Opinion does not make provisions on these two issues at this time.")*

This is independently corroborated by the separate full-text announcement page (`spc-ai-disputes-opinion-fulltext-announcement...html`), where Article 12's actual text (confirmed via direct grep against the raw file, not accepted from any secondary summary) addresses only third-party-infringement liability allocation, burden of proof, and user liability for substantially-similar output — never the threshold question of whether AI-generated content is itself copyrightable. See the milestone's own final report §O/§P for full analysis of what this means for a China Living Knowledge candidate.

## Remaining evidence limitations

- **No case number, docket identifier, or court-issued judgment document was located for either the Wuhan East Lake or Wuhan Jiang'an decision**, despite multiple independent, differently-worded search queries (Chinese and English) across a range of outlets (People's Daily/人民网, Legal Daily/法治日报, Hubei Daily/湖北日报, 极目新闻, Tencent News, Sina Finance, China News Service, China IP Lawyer Network) that consistently converge on the same underlying facts and even much of the same quoted court language — but none of which reproduce, link to, or cite a docket number or the court's own published judgment text. One bounded attempt was made against China Judgments Online (`wenshu.court.gov.cn`) generically; it returned only a JavaScript-rendering shell (a retrieval-tooling limitation per `EVIDENCE-CAPTURE-SOP.md` §1, not evidence that no judgment document exists there), and was not retried further, consistent with this SOP's established one-bounded-attempt-then-stop precedent (see e.g. the TW-TRADEMARK-1 manifest's Finding K). This is recorded as an unresolved evidence gap, not papered over — see the milestone's own final report §D/§I/§AA for the governance consequence of this gap.
- Both Wuhan sources' apparent format (party names and, for Jiang'an, the work title itself, explicitly marked "化名"/pseudonym in the source's own text; East Lake's defendant identified only as "武汉某科技有限公司") is consistent with a court-originated "典型案例" (exemplar/typical-case) public-legal-education release picked up and paraphrased by media, rather than a published, citable judgment — this is itself an evidentiary observation, not an assumption: it is the most parsimonious explanation for why no docket number is discoverable through any channel tried, but it was not independently confirmed against a court-issued "典型案例" bulletin (none was found).
- The Legal Daily and People's Daily articles are both attributed to named reporters and (for Jiang'an) a named presiding judge, and were retrieved directly from each outlet's own official domain (not an aggregator) — this is the strongest evidence available for these two decisions, but it remains a media/journalistic account of a court's reasoning, not the court's own primary text, and is classified accordingly (see evidence-tier column above). No English-language official translation was sought or relied upon for either Wuhan source; all English renderings in the milestone's report are SI8 working glosses, explicitly non-authoritative.
- The Beijing judgment PDF is the court's own official English translation, not the original Chinese judgment text — the English translation is itself an official court publication (hosted on the court's own English-language domain), not a third-party translation, and is treated as Class A on that basis; the original Chinese-language judgment was not separately retrieved or diffed against this English version in this milestone.
- The SPC Opinion's full 24-article text was not independently re-verified article-by-article beyond Articles 12 and 16 in this manifest; this is sufficient for this milestone's narrow purpose (confirming the Opinion's own explicit non-coverage of AI-output copyrightability) but would need a fuller pass if a future milestone relies on any other article of the Opinion.
- No National Copyright Administration of China (国家版权局) primary document was independently retrieved in this milestone; a media report (21jingji.com, 2026-09-08) of an NCAC official's remarks that generative-AI copyright rules will be "researched and drafted" during the 2026–2030 planning period was found but not independently primary-source-verified, and is not durably captured here — it is reported in the milestone's final report as secondary/unverified context only (§O/§P), consistent with this SOP's fail-closed discipline for weak sources.
