# Platform Rights Matrix — Schema Validation Learnings

**Purpose:** A lightweight running log kept during the first real verification pass against the Platform Rights Matrix (8 platforms: Runway, Kling, Pika, Adobe Firefly, Nano Banana, Google Veo, Midjourney, ElevenLabs). The goal of this pass is not only to populate the Matrix but to test whether the Matrix schema itself is the right canonical structure before scaling to the broader platform set. This file is the evidence base for that decision — not a permanent document, and not itself part of the Living Notebook's four-document structure.

**Status:** 8-platform pass complete, 2026-08-05. Follow-up: Kling and Pika re-verified 2026-08-05 after JD's own direct research, both moved Unconfirmed → Verified. Awaiting JD's holistic review and freeze decision.
**Related:** `PLATFORM-RIGHTS-MATRIX.md`, `08_Platform/prds/PRD_LIVING_NOTEBOOK.md` § Platform Rights Matrix

---

## How to read this file

Five running sections, appended to per platform as verification happens:

- **Fields that proved valuable** — schema fields that made a real difference during verification, with a concrete example of why.
- **Fields that were missing** — information I needed to record but had no schema field for.
- **Recurring edge cases** — situations that don't fit the clean Verified/Unconfirmed model, seen more than once.
- **Ambiguities** — places where it wasn't clear how to fill in a field, or where two reasonable people could disagree.
- **Schema friction** — anything that repeatedly slowed verification down, independent of the specific platform's facts.

Entries are tagged with the platform that surfaced them. A pattern seen across multiple platforms is noted as such explicitly, rather than left to be inferred from repetition.

## Fields that proved valuable

- **[Midjourney] The skepticism rule from Runway/Pika worked correctly by causing a check, not by causing a predetermined conclusion.** After two platforms in a row where the "Free tier = non-commercial" claim turned out false or unconfirmable, Midjourney's identical-shaped claim was checked with the same skepticism — and this time it was **true**, confirmed directly (non-paid Midjourney use is genuinely restricted to CC BY-NC 4.0). The discipline this pass has been building is "verify, don't assume either way," not "assume secondary sources are wrong" — Midjourney is the case that proves the rule isn't just pattern-matching against the Runway finding.
- **[Midjourney] A genuinely new restriction category showed up: a company-revenue threshold independent of individual plan tier.** The $1M-gross-revenue corporate-membership requirement doesn't fit "Plan Tier" cleanly — someone could hold a valid paid individual plan and still be out of compliance based on their employer's revenue, which is a fact about the *submitter's organization*, not about which Midjourney plan they personally bought. Handled this pass by writing it into Known Restrictions with an explicit SI8 Interpretation flag, but it's a different *kind* of gate than anything seen in the other 5 platforms so far — worth checking whether Reviewer Workbook already has a natural place to ask about submitter company size/revenue, since this is exactly the kind of fact that check would need to catch.
- **[Runway] "Source Wording / Confirmed Fact" forcing an actual quote, not a paraphrase, is what caught a real error.** Two secondary sources gave contradictory answers on whether Runway's Free tier permits commercial use at all (one said yes-with-watermark, one said no-commercial-use). Only fetching the actual ToS (§4.4) and quoting it directly resolved this — the field's discipline (quote, don't editorialize) is doing real work, not just paperwork.

## Fields that were missing

- **[Kling, Pika — follow-up pass] "Verification modality" is a real, missing distinction, sharper than the original "verification method" finding below.** Neither platform's ToS could be reached by this tool's automated fetch, across seven attempted URLs total. Both were still moved to `Verified` on the strength of JD's own direct browser read, independently corroborated (not just accepted) by search-indexed excerpts this pass located separately. That's legitimate primary-source verification — a human reading the actual page satisfies "SI8 has checked and cited a primary source" just as well as an automated fetch does — but it's a *different* modality than every other Verified row in this Matrix, and right now nothing in the schema records which one happened. This matters because the two modalities have different reproducibility: an automated-fetch citation can be re-run by anyone with this tool; a human-direct-read citation can only be re-verified by another human visiting the page, which may itself be access-gated differently for different people/locations (Kling and Pika are both entirely inaccessible to this tool, but were not inaccessible to JD). Candidate fix, same shape as the original finding: a `Verification Method` field or Source-field convention distinguishing "directly fetched by tooling" / "directly read by JD" / "corroborated via indexed excerpt, not independently fetched."
- **[Nano Banana] No field captures *which access surface/product path* a row's terms actually cover.** "Nano Banana" is a nickname spanning at least three distinct products — free consumer Gemini app, paid Gemini API, Vertex AI enterprise — each potentially with different terms. The Matrix schema assumes one row = one Tool with one set of terms, but this row genuinely only covers the API path; the consumer app's terms weren't checked and shouldn't be assumed identical. This is a real, structural schema-fit question, not a naming footnote: the same issue will likely recur for any tool offered through multiple access paths (API vs. app vs. enterprise platform), and today the only way to flag it is prose in the Source field, which relies on whoever writes the row remembering to do so. Candidate fix: either split multi-surface tools into separate rows per access path (e.g. "Gemini API — Nano Banana" vs. "Gemini App — Nano Banana"), or add an explicit `Access Path` field to the schema. Worth deciding deliberately at the freeze review, not by accretion.
- **[Runway] No field captures verification method / directness of sourcing.** "Source" currently answers *what* is being cited, not *how* it was checked. In practice there's a real confidence gradient: direct primary-source fetch+quote (strongest) > search-engine snippet of the primary source (strong, but not independently confirmed) > secondary/third-party legal-summary site (weakest, and — this pass already found — sometimes simply wrong). Right now all three would be written into the same `Source` field with no way to distinguish them at a glance. Candidate addition: a short `Verification Method` field, or a convention within Source ("directly fetched" vs. "corroborated via —").

## Recurring edge cases

- **[Sora, Runway, Firefly, ElevenLabs — 4 of 8 platforms] The single Status field cannot represent mixed confidence within one row, and this has now happened independently four times.** Sora (Verified for discontinuation dates, Unconfirmed for historical ToS — the original instance, already special-cased in the row before this pass started). Runway (Verified overall, but the Enterprise training-opt-out sub-claim specifically is unconfirmed). Firefly (Needs Reverification overall, with one sub-fact — training data description — actually corroborated). ElevenLabs (Verified overall for commercial tiering, but the celebrity/consent-specific restriction is only confirmed at a product-feature level, not against the actual governing policy document). **This is no longer an edge case — at half the platforms in this tranche, it's close to the norm.** Every instance was handled the same ad hoc way: write the nuance into prose and hope a future reader reads the whole cell, not just the bolded Status word. A schema that structurally supported per-fact confidence (even something as simple as inline confidence tags within a field) would remove the single largest source of "the Status word oversimplifies what's actually known" risk found in this entire pass — this is the strongest single candidate for an actual schema change, not just a documentation habit, when the freeze decision is made.
- **[Runway, Pika] A specific misconception appears to be propagating across secondary sources: conflating "Free tier has a watermark" with "Free tier prohibits commercial use."** For Runway, this claim was directly checked against the primary ToS and found **false** — commercial rights are granted on all tiers; the watermark is cosmetic, not a legal restriction. The identical claim shape was initially unconfirmable for Pika. **Follow-up (2026-08-05): now resolved, and resolved the other direction.** JD's own direct research plus an independently-located primary-source quote confirm Pika's claim is **true** — free tier genuinely is personal/non-commercial by default. So of three platforms carrying this exact claim shape (Runway, Pika, Midjourney), the record is now 2-true, 1-false. The standing rule this actually supports isn't "expect these claims to be false" — it's the narrower, correct version already noted at Midjourney: **check every instance, expect nothing either way.** Flagging the correction explicitly rather than quietly editing the earlier entry, consistent with this file's own practice of showing how a finding evolved rather than only showing where it landed.
- **[Kling] The Status enum can't distinguish "no information" from "detailed, plausible information that couldn't be independently confirmed."** A search-engine snippet of `kling.ai/docs/user-policy` yielded specific, legalese-sounding language (a written-permission gate on commercial reproduction, a labeling requirement) — much richer than a blank row, but I still couldn't directly fetch the primary source to confirm it (HTTP 446). The current schema forces this into the same `Unconfirmed` bucket as a row with zero information at all, which understates how much is actually known and risks a future reader treating "Unconfirmed" as "nothing to go on" when there's actually a specific, checkable claim sitting right there. **This is the same underlying gap as the Runway "verification method" finding, now showing up a second time in a sharper form — worth treating as one schema question, not two separate ones, when the freeze decision comes.**

## Ambiguities

- **[Google Veo] The "sibling product" inference trap.** Veo and Nano Banana share a corporate parent, likely share underlying infrastructure (Vertex AI / Gemini umbrella), and probably follow a similar terms pattern — but "probably similar" is not "confirmed," and the temptation to let a verified sibling product's terms quietly stand in for an unverified one is real and easy to miss if not named explicitly. Handled here by writing the inference into SI8 Interpretation as an explicit warning rather than upgrading Status on the strength of the analogy. Worth watching for again with any platform that has multiple related products (this will very likely recur for any company offering more than one of the 8 platforms, or a platform not yet in this Matrix at all).
- **[Runway] Partial confirmation within a single fact.** Most of the Training Data Disclosure claim was directly verified, but one sub-claim (Enterprise training opt-out) came from a secondary source and wasn't independently checked. The current schema has no clean way to mark "this field is Verified except for one sub-claim" — it's either Verified or it isn't, at the row level via Status, but individual field-level confidence isn't structurally supported. Handled this pass by writing the caveat directly into the field text, which works but relies on careful prose rather than structure.

## Schema friction

- **[Runway] Platform's own help center blocked automated fetching (HTTP 403 on `help.runwayml.com`), while the ToS on the main marketing domain (`runway.com/terms-of-use`) was fetchable.** This will likely recur — help centers and ToS pages on some platforms sit behind bot protection while marketing-adjacent legal pages don't. Not a schema problem exactly, but a real, repeatable friction point in the verification *process* worth naming so it isn't rediscovered as a surprise each time.
- **[Kling] Unlike Runway, there was no alternate domain that worked — `kling.ai` blocked the fetch (HTTP 446) with no marketing-domain fallback found.** Some platforms may simply not be independently verifiable via automated fetch at all right now, which means "verify all 8 platforms" won't produce 8 `Verified` rows regardless of effort — a real capacity limit on this workstream, not a research-effort problem.
- **[Kling] Domain-collision risk during sourcing.** Search results for "Kling" surfaced multiple similarly-named third-party sites (klingapi.com, kling3.pro, kling3.io, kling.art) that are unofficial API resellers or lookalike sites, not Kuaishou's actual platform. Easy to accidentally cite one of these as if it were primary-source Kling ToS. Worth a standing verification-process rule: confirm the actual corporate owner of a domain before treating anything on it as a primary source, not just the domain name's surface plausibility.
- **[Pika] Same domain-collision pattern as Kling** — pikalabsai.net, pika-video.com, pika-art.net, pika.group, pikalabs.io all surfaced as lookalikes to the real official `pika.art`. Two platforms in a row show this — likely a general property of popular AI tool names (SEO-driven clone/aggregator sites), not specific to either platform. Should be assumed present for every remaining platform, not rediscovered each time.
- **[Pika] A third distinct access-friction pattern: JavaScript-rendered content invisible to static HTML fetch.** Runway = bot-blocked (403). Kling = region/status-blocked (446). Pika = page loads successfully but the actual legal text is client-side rendered and never appears in the fetchable HTML — a silent failure mode, since the fetch technically "succeeds" but returns nothing useful. Three platforms, three different failure modes — access friction isn't one problem with one workaround, it's several distinct problems.
- **[Adobe Firefly] A fourth and fifth friction pattern: fetch timeout on heavy official pages, and unparseable binary content on an official legal PDF.** Two direct Adobe pages timed out outright; the official Generative AI Product-Specific Terms PDF loaded but came back as unreadable binary/compressed content this tool can't extract text from. Five platforms in, five distinct access-friction types have now shown up (bot-block, region-block, JS-rendering, timeout, unparseable-PDF) — worth treating this as an expected, recurring cost of this workstream rather than a per-platform surprise, and possibly worth a standing note in the verification process about trying a PDF-to-text intermediate step or a different fetch tool when a legal PDF is the only available primary source.
- **[Google Veo] A sixth friction pattern: content truncation on a long official page before reaching the relevant section.** The page loaded (unlike a block or timeout) but was cut off mid-document, before the actual service-coverage list. Six platforms, six distinct friction types — reinforces the earlier note that this isn't a single problem with a single fix.
- **[Pika, follow-up] A seventh friction pattern: an auth-gated early-access subdomain (HTTP 401).** `early-access.pika.art/terms-of-service` explicitly requires authentication this tool doesn't have. Seven platforms' worth of attempts, seven distinct friction types now catalogued.
- **[Kling, follow-up] A concrete, named discrepancy worth flagging directly to JD rather than silently working around: the `home.kling.ai` mirror referenced in the follow-up request does not resolve via DNS from this environment** (`getaddrinfo ENOTFOUND home.kling.ai`), and did not appear in any search results either. Could be a typo, a VPN/region-specific address, or a genuinely different environment's DNS resolution — worth JD confirming the exact hostname next time this row needs re-checking, rather than assuming the discrepancy is on this tool's side.
- **[Adobe Firefly] "Effort spent" and "verification achieved" are not the same thing, and the schema doesn't currently distinguish them.** A serious, 5-attempt reverification pass was made, and the row's Status is *still* `Needs Reverification` — correctly, since what was actually confirmed (that Adobe made an indemnification commitment, historically, per a 2023 spokesperson quote) isn't the same as confirming *current* 2026 terms. This is a good sign the Status field resisted being upgraded just because real work happened — but there's currently no way to record "reverification was attempted and partially succeeded" versus "reverification was never attempted at all." Both would currently just say `Needs Reverification` with a `Last Verified` date, and the date field alone doesn't distinguish "we tried and got partway" from "we haven't looked." The prose in Source Wording carries this distinction today, but only because it was written carefully — nothing in the schema requires or structurally supports it.
- **[Adobe Firefly] A source can be genuine, directly quote the company, and still be stale.** The Computerworld article is legitimate journalism with a real Adobe spokesperson quote — not a low-quality or unofficial source — but it's dated to a 2023 beta announcement and explicitly says terms were still "being hammered out." A naive verification pass could easily have read "direct company quote" as sufficient and marked this Verified; the thing that caught it was checking the *date* and content of the quote itself ("currently in beta"), not the source's general credibility. Source credibility and source currency are two different checks, and this pass would have failed if it only did the first one.
