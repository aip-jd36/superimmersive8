## 1. Proposition under review

> Under 17 U.S.C. § 106, a copyright owner holds the exclusive rights to reproduce their copyrighted work and to prepare derivative works based upon it (among other rights), subject to the limitations and exceptions in sections 107 through 122. Under 17 U.S.C. § 501(a), anyone who violates any of those exclusive rights without authorization is an infringer of the copyright, and may be civilly liable.

## 2. Source fidelity

Primary source (Class A, GPO-published U.S. Code, 2023 edition):

- `evidence-captures/us-copyright-third-party/uscode-2023-title17-sec106_20260918T075020Z_56f13a52.pdf`:
  > "Subject to sections 107 through 122, the owner of copyright under this title has the exclusive rights to do and to authorize any of the following: (1) to reproduce the copyrighted work in copies or phonorecords; (2) to prepare derivative works based upon the copyrighted work; (3) to distribute copies or phonorecords of the copyrighted work to the public by sale or other transfer of ownership, or by rental, lease, or lending; (4) [public performance, certain works]; (5) [public display, certain works]; (6) [digital audio transmission, sound recordings]."

- `evidence-captures/us-copyright-third-party/uscode-2023-title17-sec501_20260918T075020Z_06c69f9e.pdf`:
  > "(a) Anyone who violates any of the exclusive rights of the copyright owner as provided by sections 106 through 122 or of the author as provided in section 106A(a), or who imports copies or phonorecords into the United States in violation of section 602, is an infringer of the copyright or right of the author, as the case may be."

Tested element by element against the proposition in §1: the exclusive-rights holder ("the owner of copyright") preserved exactly; the two rights actually named in the proposition (reproduction, derivative-works preparation) are clauses (1) and (2) verbatim, not paraphrased or broadened; the "subject to sections 107 through 122" limitations clause preserved verbatim, disclosing (not characterizing) that exceptions exist; the infringement/liability consequence ("is an infringer... may be civilly liable") tracks §501(a)'s own text, cross-referencing back to §106 exactly as the statute itself does. **The proposition deliberately omits §106's remaining rights (3)-(6)** (distribution, public performance, public display, digital audio transmission) — a scope decision, not an evidentiary gap: reproduction and derivative-works preparation are the two rights most directly implicated by an AI system generating new video content that visually or substantively resembles a pre-existing third-party work; the other four rights concern distinct activities (physical/digital distribution, live performance, display, transmission) not the core fact pattern this candidate targets. This mirrors the Trademark package's own disclosed, reasoned narrowing of §1125(a)(1) to omit clause (B).

No instance of the proposition being stated more strongly than the source was found: no infringement is asserted as established; no defendant/actor is named; no third-party work is characterized as protected; "subject to... limitations" is disclosed generally, not resolved.

## 4. Output vs. training-data boundary

This candidate concerns exclusively **output-side** exposure: the user's own AI-generated video itself containing, reproducing, or being a derivative work based on pre-existing third-party protected expression. It does **not** concern, and this package takes no position on, whether or how an AI model's own training process implicates §106/§501 (the Getty v. Stability AI/Andersen v. Stability AI-style model-training-reproduction theory). This boundary is preserved independently three ways: (a) the candidate proposition itself (§1) states a general rights/infringement structure applicable to any reproduction or derivative work, without reference to how the allegedly-infringing copy was produced; (b) Reviewer Workbook Domain I explicitly brackets training-data liability as a separate, un-auditable "residual risk" that never blocks a positive I01 outcome (`SI8-Reviewer-Manual-v0.3.md`, Domain I); (c) no training-data source was captured or relied upon for this candidate's evidence (§3, `MANIFEST.md`).

> Under U.S. federal copyright law (17 U.S.C. § 106), the owner of a copyrighted work holds exclusive rights to reproduce it and to prepare derivative works based on it, among other rights, subject to statutory limitations and exceptions. ## 7. Taxonomy — independent adjudication (T1–T4)

**T1 — New `GoalCategory`.** A new value (proposed: `third_party_copyright`) representing the distinct explicit user ask: "my AI-generated/AI-assisted output contains, reproduces, adapts, or resembles someone else's copyrighted material — what should I understand?"
1. *UserGoal meaning:* an explicit ask about a third party's rights being implicated by the user's own output — the mirror image of `copyright_ownership`'s "is this my copyright," never the same question.
2. *Honest representation:* yes — matches the user's actual words most directly, without requiring Extraction to relabel the ask under an existing, differently-scoped topic.
3. *Fabrication risk:* none — reaches the existing, unmodified `lookupTopicClaims` exact-topic path exactly as `likeness`/`copyright_ownership`/`trademark` already do.
4. *Over-retrieval of unrelated Copyright propositions:* no — a distinct topic string keeps `copyrightability`/`copyright_ownership`'s three-plus-one claims out of this topic's own retrieval set, and vice versa.
5. *Under-retrieval:* no — a dedicated topic guarantees retrieval on an explicit ask, rather than depending on a relationship that could be missed or mis-triggered.
6. *Explicit-vs-discovered preserved:* yes — explicit-goal-only (§10 below).
7. *Track C provenance:* not implicated (no relationship, no discovered-topic routing).
8. *Scales to future proposition families:* yes — a dedicated topic can host future related third-party-copyright propositions (e.g. a future fair-use-framing claim) the same way `copyrightability` already hosts three claims under one topic.
9. *Represents user intent, not merely legal doctrine:* yes — the justification is not "different statute" but a genuinely different underlying question in the user's own mind (my rights in my work vs. exposure from someone else's work appearing in my output) — independently confirmed by testing three plausible user intents: *"What copyright issue should I understand if my commercial includes a scene that resembles someone else's movie/artwork?"*; *"Can I recreate a famous painting/character in my AI video?"*; *"Is my AI video a derivative work of someone else's film?"* — all three are direct, explicit asks about a third party's rights, none reduces to "is my own output copyrightable."
10. *Necessity:* minimal-cost, one enum-array entry, exact precedent shape (`trademark`, `likeness`, `copyright_ownership`).

**T2 — Fold into an existing Copyright `GoalCategory`** (`copyright_ownership` or `copyrightability`).
1-2. Would require Extraction to force-fit "does my output contain someone else's material" into a topic semantically defined as being about *the user's own* authorship/ownership — dishonest relative to `TOPIC-RELATIONSHIPS.md`'s own Core Principle ("a user's captured goal stays exactly what the user asked").
4. Over-retrieval risk is real and asymmetric: folding this need into `copyright_ownership` would make COPY-004's own ownership-vs-ToS-permission claim a retrieval co-candidate whenever a user asks about third-party material in their output, and vice versa — two questions with zero conceptual overlap presented as if related.
8. Poor scalability — future third-party-copyright propositions would compound the conflation.
**Rejected** — same reasoning the read-only diagnostic already found (existing Copyright LK is entirely "can I claim copyright in my own output," never "does my output implicate someone else's"), independently re-confirmed here via the intent-testing method in T1.

**T3 — Existing `GoalCategory` + `KnowledgeTopic`/`TopicRelationship`** (the Article-50/`ai_content_transparency` shape: a `KNOWLEDGE_ONLY_TOPICS` addition, reached only via a governed relationship from another explicit goal, never directly).
This shape exists specifically for topics a user would never directly, explicitly ask about — topics that are relevant *context* for interpreting a different explicit goal (EU AI Act disclosure is never itself a direct ask; it's discovered while discussing `commercial_use`). The three intents tested in T1 above are **direct, explicit asks** — a user who says "does my video recreate a copyrighted character" is not asking about `commercial_use` and incidentally needing this surfaced; they are asking this question itself. **Rejected** — the same reasoning `FGR_021` used to reject this shape for Trademark applies with equal, independently-confirmed force here: this domain is directly askable, not merely relevant-context for another goal.

**T4 — Another existing generic representation already sufficient.**
Checked `third_party_source_rights`: presupposes a named, licensed stock-media provider relationship (`which_provider`, `editorial_designation_confirmed` are its own dependencies) — semantically inapplicable to emergent or uncredited resemblance to a third party's work with no stock-provider relationship in the fact pattern at all. Checked whether applicability/dependency additions to an *existing* claim could represent this need without a new topic: no — applicability/dependency mechanisms narrow *when* an already-topic-matched claim applies; they cannot change *what topic* a claim is retrieved under, so they cannot substitute for topic identity. **Rejected** — no existing generic representation fits without semantic distortion (already demonstrated in T2's testing).

**Decision: T1 — NEW `GoalCategory` (`third_party_copyright`, proposed name, not final until PM Adoption).** Independently re-derived via the same head-to-head method `FGR_021` used for Trademark, arrived at for reasons specific to this domain's own semantic shape (a directly-askable question about a third party's rights, structurally distinct from every existing Copyright topic), not by inheriting "Trademark chose a new category" or by the weaker "different statute" reasoning this milestone specifically warned against.

**`KnowledgeTopic` identity:** because `KnowledgeTopic = GoalCategory | KNOWLEDGE_ONLY_TOPICS` is a union (`retrieval-engine/types.ts`), adding `third_party_copyright` to `GOAL_CATEGORIES` automatically makes it a valid `TopicClaim.topic` value — one governed-data addition satisfies both roles, exactly mirroring `trademark`'s/`likeness`'s/`copyright_ownership`'s own shape (never the `KNOWLEDGE_ONLY_TOPICS`-only, relationship-routed shape, which T3 above confirms does not fit here). Enables direct exact-topic retrieval via the existing, unmodified `lookupTopicClaims` path. **No `TopicRelationship` is required or proposed** — not a mechanical carry-over from Trademark's own conclusion, but the independently-re-tested result of T3 above.

**Proposed candidate dependency, if any:** `unauthorized_reproduction_or_derivative_use_of_third_party_work` — whether the specific output, in fact, reproduces or is a derivative work based on identifiable third-party protected expression, without authorization. A deliberate **composite** representation (disclosed as such, not a hidden oversimplification) standing in for the practical bottom-line characterization a user's real question actually turns on.

**The key distinguishing precedent test, run before choosing D0 or D1:** `CLAIM-COPY-004-v1` (ownership vs. ToS-permission) is Adopted, CRC-eligible, with **zero** applicability requirements and **zero** dependencies — proving a zero-dependency Copyright-domain claim is an approved, safe pattern reaching `directly_relevant` whenever retrieved. But COPY-004's proposition is a **closed-form definitional distinction** ("owning a copyright and having ToS permission are different things") that is fully true and complete on its own — no project-specific fact could make it non-true or misleadingly incomplete, so nothing is lost by reaching `directly_relevant` (no hedge needed). By contrast, this candidate's proposition — like `CLAIM-TRADEMARK-US-LANHAM-CONFUSION-001-v1`'s — is a **liability-exposure rule**: its practical relevance to the user depends entirely on an unstated, unresolved project-specific fact (does the output actually reproduce/derive from a specific third party's protected expression, and was it authorized). Presenting a liability-exposure rule via the `directly_relevant` path (no hedge, no flagged open characterization) risks the user over-reading "you have exclusive rights and violating them is infringement" as having answered their real question ("is *my* video okay"), when in fact the one fact that actually matters was never examined.

Applying the 13-question test:
1. *Exact project-state proposition:* whether the output reproduces/derives from identifiable third-party protected expression without authorization.
2/3. *CRC-permitted conclusion that changes on resolution:* none — CRC never concludes infringement/copying/substantial-similarity/derivative-status regardless of this dependency's presence or state (the fixed, domain-blind `rules.ts` Composition templates already structurally prevent that, confirmed by the same direct-source-trace method `CPR_027` used for Trademark's own dependency, re-applied here rather than assumed transferable).
4. *Materially improves the user's bounded answer:* yes, specifically via the Case-3B closing-sentence content (naming the actual open characterization) rather than a generic jurisdiction-only hedge.
5. *Duplicates a CAA evidence-review item:* semantically overlaps with, but does not operationally duplicate, Domain I01's own direct-content-review determination — the same relationship Trademark's dependency has to Domain I03, not disqualifying.
6/7/8. *Requires direct evidence review, human judgment, and is a legal characterization:* yes to all three — exactly parallel to I01's own "Weak evidence" downgrade of unverified self-disclosure.
9/10. *Askable / evidence-only:* not askable (§9 below); evidence-only.
11. *General proposition remains useful without it:* yes — §1's proposition is fully true and useful with zero dependencies.
12. *BI needs it to remain "not-fabricating":* no — that guarantee is structural, independent of dependency count.
13. *Independent semantic/governance purpose, not merely richer prose:* the purpose is the same *class* of purpose `CPR_027` identified for Trademark's own dependency — Reviewer/HRR-facing aggregate-signal honesty (forcing `relevant_applicability_unresolved` rather than `directly_relevant` for a *liability-exposure*-shaped proposition) and CRC disclosure specificity — not a Composition-safety requirement, and not adopted merely because Trademark has one.

**A material disanalogy to Trademark, weighed honestly, not glossed over:** Trademark's dependency collapses **one literal statutory disjunctive clause** (§1125(a)(1)(A) is a single sentence with "or" connectors) — a direct statutory mirror. Copyright infringement's actual determination is **not** stated as one clause; it is a judicially-elaborated, multi-element test (protectable expression exists + copying in fact + substantial similarity of protected elements + no authorization/no applicable exception) spread across §106, §107 (fair use), and case law, none of which this candidate's bounded evidence captured (§3). Collapsing all of that into one dependency string is therefore an **SI8-authored composite**, not a statutory mirror — a materially different, and by itself weaker, justification than Trademark's own. This package does not paper over that difference: the dependency proposed here is disclosed explicitly as a composite standing in for "the practical bottom-line characterization," not a claim that copyright infringement reduces to one clean fact.

**Decision: D1 — retain exactly one dependency** (`unauthorized_reproduction_or_derivative_use_of_third_party_work`), reached not by inheriting Trademark's shape but by an independent proposition-shape test (liability-exposure rule vs. closed-form definitional distinction, using `CLAIM-COPY-004-v1`'s own zero-dependency precedent as the contrasting data point, not merely Trademark's one-dependency precedent) — while disclosing explicitly, per the Trademark package's own precedent of naming what is deliberately excluded, that this dependency is a **composite**, not a statutory-clause mirror.

**Explicitly excluded from the dependency model** (mirroring the Trademark package's own exclusion discipline):
- **Ownership/validity of the third-party copyright** — a separate legal question (whether *anyone* owns a valid copyright in the referenced work at all), not an element this candidate's proposition needs resolved to be true or useful; modeling it as a dependency would also duplicate the existing `copyrightability` topic's own subject matter, applied to someone else's work instead of the user's.
- **Authorization/license sufficiency, standing alone** — folded into the single composite dependency above (the composite already asks "without authorization"), not tracked as a second, separate dependency — avoiding the "arbitrary dependency fragmentation" pitfall `FGR_021`'s adversarial review specifically checked for.
- **Fair use / other §107-122 exceptions** — explicitly disclosed as existing (§1's own "subject to... limitations" clause) but not modeled as a dependency; resolving whether an exception applies is itself a legal characterization at least as complex as the underlying infringement question, and is not needed for the general proposition to remain true and useful.
- **Registration status** — copyright protection (unlike registration-dependent remedies such as statutory damages) does not require registration to exist or to be infringed; not a dependency of this proposition.
