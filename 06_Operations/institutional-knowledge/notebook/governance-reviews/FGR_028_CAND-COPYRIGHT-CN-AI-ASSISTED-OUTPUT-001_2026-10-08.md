## 6. Exact proposition supported by the evidence

> In Chinese litigation, when courts assess whether AI-assisted or AI-generated content is protected by copyright, official Supreme People's Court guidance (the People's Courts IP Judicial Protection Implementation Plan, 2026–2030) directs courts to comprehensively consider the human user's specific input instructions and the concrete process of selection and modification, to determine whether the resulting content reflects the human's own original creative choices and expression. Applying reasoning along these lines, particular Chinese courts (reported lower-court decisions from Wuhan) have found copyright protection where they concluded the human contributor exercised substantive, continuous creative control over the final expression and treated the AI tool as an instrument rather than an autonomous author. However, the Supreme People's Court has twice stated, in two separate official explanatory publications (2026-09-07 and 2026-09-09), that views on the copyrightability of AI-generated content remain deeply divided among both scholars and practitioners, and that its own September 2026 Opinion on AI-related disputes does not establish a rule on this question. There is no uniform national rule on AI-content copyrightability in China at this time.

This is the controlling candidate text — subject to independent re-derivation, not automatic carry-forward, at a future PM Adoption review.

## 7. Explicit statements the evidence does NOT support

Per §E, none of the following may be governed: "AI-generated content is copyrightable in China"; "China recognizes copyright in AI-generated works"; "AI-generated works are protected when prompts are sufficiently creative"; "Human control makes AI output copyrightable"; "Substantial human contribution satisfies Chinese copyright law"; "Using AI as a tool means the output is human-authored"; "The Wuhan test is the Chinese national copyrightability test"; "The Jiang'an test is the Chinese national copyrightability test." Also not supported: any claim that the Implementation Plan itself resolves rights-attribution (its own text says this is still "explored/studied"); any claim that the Wuhan cases are citable, appealable-reviewed, or precedential beyond their own reported fact patterns; any claim that a national judicial interpretation exists on this question (none does — Article 12 of the actual Opinion addresses a different question, third-party infringement).

## 8. Treatment of the SPC's national-unsettled position

Treated as **authoritative, load-bearing evidence**, not a caveat. Two independent official SPC statements, from two different SPC offices, on two different dates, are the single strongest, most Class-A-certain finding in this entire evidentiary record — stronger, in fact, than the Wuhan case evidence (which is Class D-adjacent and has no docket-level confirmation). The proposition in §6 is built so that the uncertainty is a sentence of the proposition's own primary content, not a disclaimer appended after.

## 9. Is the uncertainty structurally inseparable from the proposition?

Yes. Any rendering of this proposition that drops the final two sentences (SPC non-decision; no uniform national rule) would misrepresent the Wuhan fact patterns as if they were a settled national test — exactly the false generalization §E prohibits. The uncertainty is not severable from the "particular courts have found..." sentence; it must travel with it in every realization. This is architecturally equivalent to how CPR_032/CPR_033 made temporal qualifications (effective dates) inseparable from the EU Article 50(2) proposition's own primary sentence rather than a trailing note — the same "state it in the sentence that will actually be rendered" pattern, reused here for a different kind of qualification (legal-uncertainty rather than temporal) but the identical generic mechanism: **fixed, date/fact-leading governed wording, not new runtime logic.** No new architecture is required to make uncertainty load-bearing — it already is, by virtue of being literally inside `crc_candidate_statement`, which `rules.ts`'s Case-3B template (and any stronger composition tier) quotes verbatim and never truncates or paraphrases (confirmed by this session's own repeated, independent tracing of that exact code path for the California and EU Article 50(2) claims).

## 14. Dependency Challenge: D0 vs. D1 vs. alternative

**Recommended: D1 = `['human_contribution_description']`.** Chosen not for Taiwan symmetry but because: (a) it independently passes the admission test on the mechanism's own confirmed properties (§13); (b) it provides a second, independent layer of boundedness beyond the proposition's own self-qualifying text — belt-and-suspenders, consistent with this session's general posture of preferring more conservative dependency models for claims touching genuinely contested/unsettled doctrine (as opposed to Taiwan, where TIPO's own official interpretation affirmatively settles the national rule, leaving only the project-specific-sufficiency question unresolved); (c) it costs nothing architecturally — reuses the existing, already-safe, already-tested mechanism with zero code change. D0 was not rejected as unsafe (§9 shows it would also be defensible), but D1 is the smaller-risk choice given the comparison in (b), and the user's own framing in §H ("If that means a permanent Case3B or equivalent... say so explicitly") signals this outcome is expected and acceptable, not something to engineer around.
