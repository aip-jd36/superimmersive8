> "2. ## 2. Clause-by-clause derivation of the regulated obligation

**Regulated actor (§F):** "Providers" — Article 3(3)'s own term, exclusively used throughout paragraph 2. Never "deployer" (the Article 50(4)/50(3) term), "operator" (Article 74, not used in Article 50 at all), "user," "creator," "advertiser," "agency," or "client." This paragraph's actor vocabulary is independently re-confirmed as internally consistent and never conflated with Article 50(4)'s "deployer" anywhere in the captured text.

**Governed subject / content scope (§G/§H):** "AI systems, including general-purpose AI systems, generating synthetic audio, image, video or text content." Four modalities — **audio, image, video, AND text** — materially broader than Article 50(4), which covers only image/audio/video (no text). This is a genuine, source-grounded difference, not an oversight: Article 50(4)'s "deep fake" definition (Article 3(60)) is itself expressly limited to "image, audio or video content"; Article 50(2) has no such limitation and explicitly includes text.

**Core obligation and the marking/detectability relationship (§I/§J/§K):** The statute states ONE "shall ensure" duty with two conjunctive technical properties the output must have: (a) "marked in a machine-readable format," and (b) "detectable as artificially generated or manipulated." Grammatically and structurally this is a single statutory sentence imposing one unified obligation ("ensure A and B"), not two independent shall-clauses triggered by different conditions. Per this corpus's own atomicity discipline ("one clause = one claim," applied throughout — e.g. the single-sentence Lanham and California propositions), **this review finds NO basis to split marking and detectability into two separate propositions.** They remain analytically distinct technical concepts (marking describes the mechanism; detectability describes the required outcome/property) and the governed proposition below preserves that distinction in wording, but governs them as one claim, because the statute itself conditions both on the exact same trigger and the exact same actor with no independent branching.

**Technical-feasibility qualification (§L):** The obligation to ensure "effective, interoperable, robust and reliable" technical solutions is itself expressly bounded "as far as this is technically feasible, taking into account the specificities and limitations of various types of content, the costs of implementation and the generally acknowledged state of the art, as may be reflected in relevant technical standards." This is not a side note — it is a load-bearing qualifier on the entire obligation, confirming this is not an absolute, unconditional technical mandate. This qualifier is independently significant for §9 (dependency adjudication) below: it is itself a multi-factor evaluative standard, not a fact.

**Exclusions (§H, continued):** Three, source-stated, disjunctive: (i) "the AI systems perform an assistive function for standard editing," (ii) the AI system "do[es] not substantially alter the input data provided by the deployer or the semantics thereof," (iii) the law-enforcement authorization exception (verbatim-parallel to Article 50(1)'s and 50(4)'s own law-enforcement carve-outs). All three attach to the **AI system's own technical operation**, not to the user's project — see §9 below for why this matters to dependency classification.

**Effective date / temporal structure (§M, developed fully in §8 below):** Materially more complex than a single future date. Two source-stated dates apply to two different populations of AI systems.

**One further, independently-derived distinction not previously stated in `FGR_019`:** Article 50(4)'s "deployer" actor class is structurally broad — almost any user of an AI system under their own authority qualifies (Article 3(4): "using an AI system under its authority"), meaning SI8's typical CRC user (a commercial AI-video creator using a third-party tool) plausibly **is** a deployer for that claim's purposes. Article 50(2)'s "provider" actor class is structurally narrow — it requires having developed the AI system (or had it developed) and placed it on the market or put it into service **under one's own name or trademark**. ## 4. Proposition derivation (from zero, not copied from `FGR_019`'s own prior language, which did not draft a 50(2) proposition)

> Under Article 50(2) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (the AI Act), a provider of an AI system, including a general-purpose AI system, that generates synthetic audio, image, video, or text content must ensure that the outputs of that AI system are marked in a machine-readable format and are detectable as artificially generated or manipulated. ## 8. Temporal status — independently derived, tested against the California date-leading precedent

Two source-stated dates, confirmed by Class A/B primary text (§1 above), govern two different populations of the same obligation:

- **General application date: 2 August 2026** (the Article 50 transparency obligations' own base application date, per the Commission Guidelines and EPRS briefing, both already in the evidence package — confirmed consistent, not newly asserted by this review). **This date has already passed** as of this review (2026-10-02) — Article 50(2) is, in general, already applicable law today, unlike the California claim's wholly-future date.
- **Transitional date: 2 December 2026** — Article 111(4), applicable only to providers of systems "placed on the market before 2 August 2026," giving them until this later date to come into compliance. **This date has not yet passed** as of this review.

**Test against the California date-leading pattern:** the pattern (stating the effective date plainly rather than asserting present-tense operativeness) remains sufficient here, but the structure is a genuine step more complex than California's single future date — it requires stating **two** dates and **which population each attaches to**, not one. The draft proposition in §4 does this directly ("the obligation otherwise applies from 2 August 2026" / "a provider... placed on the market before 2 August 2026 has until 2 December 2026"), producing a sentence that remains accurate whenever read (before, between, or after both dates) without requiring date-interpolation, a current-date check, or any new runtime temporal-gating mechanism. **No new temporal architecture is required** — this is a confirmation and a modest stress-test of the existing wording-only pattern's sufficiency, not a new finding of insufficiency.

**One genuine limitation, recorded, not solved:** CRC has no way to know, and should not ask the user to self-attest, **which of the two populations a specific AI tool's provider falls into** (i.e., whether the specific tool was "placed on the market" before or after 2 August 2026) — this is Provider Evidence (§14 below), not a CRC-askable project fact, and is not represented as a dependency for that reason.

## 14. Provider evidence vs. project evidence vs. governed law — explicit separation preserved

## 16. Prohibited conclusions

CRC must not state or imply: that a specific AI system, provider, or output satisfies Article 50(2); that the user (or their organization) is the statutory "provider" (absent `provider_status_confirmed` resolving otherwise, which no current mechanism can do); that a specific technical marking or detection mechanism is machine-readable, detectable, effective, interoperable, robust, reliable, or technically feasible in the statute's own sense; that a specific case falls within the assistive-editing or non-substantial-alteration exclusions; that a specific AI system was or was not placed on the market before 2 August 2026, or that the 2 August 2026 or 2 December 2026 date currently binds a specific project; that Article 2(1)(a)/(c)'s territorial nexus is established for a specific project merely because the user named the European Union as assessment jurisdiction or a distribution territory; that Article 50(2) and Article 50(4) are the same duty, or that satisfying/being exempt from one satisfies the other; that the user must personally add a watermark or implement technical marking (the duty, where it attaches at all, falls on the AI system's provider, not on SI8's typical CRC user — §3); that visible, human-facing labeling and Article 50(2)'s machine-readable technical marking are the same thing; that a provider's own compliance claim, or the presence of Content Credentials or similar metadata, establishes compliance; that the absence of visible marking establishes a violation by anyone; or that satisfying, or being exempt from, this obligation establishes broader legal compliance, copyright clearance, provider/platform permission, or overall commercial readiness.
