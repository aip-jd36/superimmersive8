## 1. What this claim governs, kept distinct from Article 50(4)

Confirmed directly from the adopted proposition text: Article 50(2) is a **provider-side** duty — "a provider of an AI system... must ensure that the outputs of that AI system are marked in a machine-readable format and are detectable as artificially generated or manipulated." It is NOT a "disclose to viewers" duty, NOT a "deep fake" characterization, and does not use Article 3(60)'s "deep fake" term anywhere. This is structurally and textually distinct from Article 50(4) (deployer-side, human-facing, deep-fake-specific disclosure). The adopted proposition's own closing scope-limitation sentence and the ledger's "Related" field both state this non-overlap explicitly; this review independently confirms it is accurate, not merely asserted, and does not find a basis to describe the two provisions as "the same transparency duty" anywhere in the governed text.

## 7. Principle 3 — independently re-derived for THIS claim's own subject, not inherited from CPR_026

**Tested against each named Principle 3 subject individually, not collapsed into one label:**

The textual overlap that drove `CPR_026`'s own careful, novel Principle 3 analysis for the sibling claim is **structurally absent** here.
4. **Political persuasion** — No. No reference to political content, persuasion, or any content-category nexus of that kind.

**Independent resolution, applying the same axis `CPR_026` §I identified (does publishing this proposition itself constitute, produce, or facilitate deception, or does it inform against it) rather than inheriting that finding's conclusion:** this proposition states a provider-side technical transparency mandate whose entire function is to make AI-generated/manipulated content more detectable and more identifiable as such — the precise opposite of facilitating deception. **Finalized CRC Candidate Statement:**
> Under Article 50(2) of the EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689), a provider of an AI system — the company or organization that develops or publishes the AI tool, not a stock-footage or asset vendor, and not the person using the tool to create content — must ensure that outputs the system generates (audio, image, video, or text) are marked in a machine-readable format and detectable as artificially generated or manipulated, subject to statutory technical-feasibility, assistive-editing, and law-enforcement qualifications. The obligation applies from 2 August 2026, except that a provider of a system placed on the market before that date has until 2 December 2026 to come into compliance. This duty falls on the AI system's provider, not on the person using the system to create content.

**Finalized CRC Publication Scope:**
> CRC may state that EU Regulation 2024/1689, Article 50(2) (together with Article 111(4), as inserted by the Digital Omnibus, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744) imposes a technical machine-readable-marking and detectability duty on providers of AI systems that generate synthetic audio, image, video, or text content, subject to the statute's own technical-feasibility, assistive-editing/non-substantial-alteration, and law-enforcement qualifications, and that this duty is distinct from, and does not depend on, Article 50(4)'s separate deployer-facing deep-fake disclosure duty. CRC must not state or imply: that a specific AI system, provider, or output satisfies Article 50(2); that the user or their organization is the statutory "provider" of the AI system they used; that any specific technical marking or detection mechanism (including watermarking, Content Credentials, or metadata) is machine-readable, detectable, effective, interoperable, robust, reliable, or technically feasible in the statute's own sense; that a specific case falls within the assistive-editing or non-substantial-alteration exclusions; that a specific AI system was or was not placed on the market before 2 August 2026, or that either date currently binds a specific project; that Article 2(1)'s territorial scope attaches to a specific project merely because the user named the European Union as assessment jurisdiction or a distribution territory; that Article 50(2) and Article 50(4) are the same duty, or that satisfying or being exempt from one satisfies the other; that the user must personally add a watermark or implement technical marking; that a provider's own compliance claim establishes compliance; that the absence of visible marking establishes a violation by anyone; or that satisfying, or being exempt from, this obligation establishes broader legal compliance, copyright clearance, provider/platform permission, or overall commercial readiness.
