## Formal Governance Review #1 — `CAND-STOCK-EDITORIAL-001`

**1. Exact candidate wording reviewed** (verbatim, Part 2 §11):
> "A stock-media provider's standard license for content it designates 'Editorial' (or an equivalent editorial-use-only classification) authorizes use for descriptive, newsworthy, or public-interest purposes. Under that standard license, this does not include advertising, promotional, endorsement, or merchandising use. Some providers offer a separate, provider-specific process to authorize such use for a given asset — this proposition does not itself confirm whether such authorization exists for any particular asset or project."

**2. Research artifact section used:** `STOCK-MEDIA-EDITORIAL-USE-CANDIDATE-RESEARCH-2026.md`, Part 1 §5–§9/§20 and Part 2 §2–§13/§18/§21.

**3. Accuracy assessment:** Strong overall. Every material clause traces to hardened evidence; no clause overstates provider uniformity or misrepresents any provider's actual position.

**4. Clause-by-clause support:**
- "authorizes use for descriptive, newsworthy, or public-interest purposes" — SUPPORTED, all four (Getty/iStock Tier 1, Adobe Tier 2/3, Shutterstock Tier 1).
- "does not include advertising, promotional, endorsement, or merchandising use" — SUPPORTED for Getty/iStock (Tier 1, near-verbatim enumerated lists including these exact terms), SUPPORTED for Adobe (Tier 2/3), **functionally SUPPORTED but not verbatim-term-matched for Shutterstock** (Tier 1 for the general commercialize/sell/promote/advertise-vs-editorial distinction; "endorsement"/"merchandising" specifically rest on Tier 3/Official-Secondary corroboration, not a direct Shutterstock quote). Not a truthfulness problem — the claim doesn't assert verbatim provider language, only functional effect — but worth disclosing precisely in provenance (see item 20).
- "Some providers offer a separate... process... does not itself confirm whether such authorization exists" — SUPPORTED and carefully hedged; correctly non-universal (doesn't claim iStock or Adobe have one).

**5. Getty evidence assessment: STRONG.** Five independent fetches across the whole research program, Tier 1 direct fetch of both the primary EULA and the Rights & Clearance page, consistent substance despite unstable section-number labels (labels don't affect substantive confidence).

**6. iStock evidence assessment: STRONG.** Most stable provider researched — Tier 1, consistent "Last Updated: July 2026" across three independent fetches, near-identical clause structure to Getty confirmed independently.

**7. Adobe evidence assessment: QUALIFIED — and deliberately assessed independently of `-002`'s weaker Adobe finding, per instruction.** For this specific license-scope proposition (not release-relatedness, not the exception mechanism), Adobe has one Tier 2 direct fetch (Phase 0) plus two independent, consistent Tier 3 `WebSearch` corroborations of the same core enumerated-use language, including the "commercial blogs" and documentary/educational carve-out language this candidate's sibling scenario-testing relied on. This is meaningfully stronger than `-002`'s near-total Tier 3 reliance for release-relatedness — QUALIFIED, not INSUFFICIENT, for this narrower proposition specifically.

**8. Shutterstock evidence assessment: QUALIFIED.** Tier 1 for the core functional definition (direct fetch, official `submit.shutterstock.com` domain). Official Secondary, not Verified Primary, for the exact restriction wording and precise exception-mechanism mechanics. The primary customer-facing license agreement remains completely unverified — 0 of 7 attempted fetches succeeded across two full research sessions, a confirmed structural access limitation, not a one-off.

**9. Cross-provider synthesis assessment: legitimate.** Structural commonality (shared functional shape: editorial grant excludes advertising/promotional-type use) is well-evidenced across all four; the claim does not assert identical contract language and doesn't need to. Shutterstock's structurally different drafting approach (defined-term-and-negation vs. enumerated list) doesn't change the *truth* of the functional proposition, only the contract mechanism — the claim correctly abstracts at the level it can honestly support.

**10. Commercial-vs-promotional boundary assessment: passes cleanly.** The word "commercial" never appears as a governing term. Uses only "advertising, promotional, endorsement, or merchandising" — does not imply every paid use, every business use, every corporate-website use, or documentary use is prohibited. This is the single most important test given the whole research program's purpose, and it passes without qualification.

**11. Exception/authorization boundary assessment: passes.** Does not falsely imply no exceptions exist (Getty/Shutterstock's real mechanisms are acknowledged in principle); does not falsely imply exceptions always exist ("some providers," not "providers"); appropriately defers exception *mechanics* to provider-specific knowledge rather than forcing detail into the structural claim.

**12. Project-specific determination boundary assessment: passes cleanly.** Never asserts a specific user's asset is Editorial, that a specific use violates a license, that a user lacks authorization, that a campaign is prohibited, or that any license is invalid. Explicitly disclaims confirming authorization status for "any particular asset or project."

**13. Atomicity assessment: ACCEPTABLY COMPOUND, not strictly atomic, and correctly so.** The license-scope-excludes-advertising/promotional clause and the exceptions-may-exist clause are technically two assertions, but removing the second would make the first misleading (implying an absolute rule) — this mirrors CLAIM-COPY-004's own accepted precedent of necessary compounding for truthfulness, not scope-creep bundling of unrelated facts. Splitting the exceptions clause into its own claim was already tried and rejected in Phase 1B (`-003`, R4) for good, evidenced reasons — removing it from `-001` now would leave that content ungoverned entirely and recreate the exact "categorical exclusion" misrepresentation this whole research program exists to avoid. Do not split.

**14. CRC/Commercial Assurance boundary assessment: passes.** States the general rule only; never collapses provider, exact asset, classification, license/download record, agreement version, intended use, authorization status, or releases into an answer.

**15. Source-quality assessment:** Getty STRONG, iStock STRONG, Adobe QUALIFIED (for this claim specifically), Shutterstock QUALIFIED. Combined basis: sufficient for a hedged, functionally-framed cross-provider synthesis — two STRONG legs, two QUALIFIED (not INSUFFICIENT) legs, no CONTRADICTED findings anywhere.
