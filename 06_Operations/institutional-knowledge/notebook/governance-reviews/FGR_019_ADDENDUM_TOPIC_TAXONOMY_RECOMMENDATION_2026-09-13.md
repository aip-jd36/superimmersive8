## 1. Naming principles applied

Derived from the existing `GOAL_CATEGORIES` taxonomy's own established style (`commercial_use`, `copyright_ownership`, `copyrightability`, `likeness`, `third_party_source_rights`) and `KnowledgeTopic`'s own module-header discipline (`lib/retrieval-engine/types.ts`, KnowledgeTopic Foundation milestone, 2026-09-13): snake_case, generic subject-matter noun phrase — never a statute citation, never a compliance verdict, never phrased as a user's own question (that is `GoalCategory`'s job, not `KnowledgeTopic`'s).

Rejected naming patterns, with reasons:
- **Statute-specific** (`article_50_4`, `eu_ai_act_deepfake_disclosure`, `art50_4_audiovisual`): breaks the existing style outright (no existing `GoalCategory` is statute-named), and would need a new value for every future jurisdiction's equivalent rule even though the underlying subject is the same.
- **Too broad** (`ai_regulation`, `regulatory_compliance`, `content_authenticity`): would collapse unrelated future obligations (data-protection, product-safety, general non-AI authenticity/provenance schemes) into one topic, exactly the "names so broad they collapse unrelated future obligations" failure this milestone's own charge warns against.
- **Implies user intent** (anything phrased as a question, e.g. `is_this_ai_disclosed`): confuses `KnowledgeTopic` (what governed knowledge is about) with `GoalCategory` (what a user explicitly wants help with) — the exact conflation the KnowledgeTopic Foundation milestone exists to prevent.
- **Implies compliance** (`ai_disclosure_compliance`, `deepfake_clearance`): a topic label must never assert or imply a compliance verdict — matching FGR_019 §10/§11's own explicit "never a compliance conclusion" discipline, extended here to the topic label itself, not just the claim's prose.
- **Reuses "synthetic" as the anchor term** (`synthetic_content_disclosure`, `synthetic_media_disclosure`): rejected specifically because this codebase already uses "synthetic" as the anchor term for a *different, narrower, already-governed* concept — `CLAIM-NY-SYNTHETIC-PERFORMER-DISCLOSURE-001-v1`'s own domain (a real, identifiable person's likeness/voice being synthetically replicated, topic `likeness`). Reusing "synthetic" here risks a future reader conflating "AI-generated content in general" with "a synthetic replica of a specific real person" — two materially different legal questions FGR_019 §8 itself distinguishes ("a project can have full likeness clearance... and still face an undisclosed Article 50(4) deep-fake-labeling obligation, and conversely a project with no likeness issue at all... could still trigger Article 50(4)").

## 3. Recommendation

**Recommended identifier: `ai_content_transparency`.**

**Recommended human-readable label: "AI Content Transparency."**

**Rationale for choosing #2 over #1 (`ai_content_disclosure`), both of which pass every test in §4/§5 below identically:** #2 is the more directly evidence-grounded choice — it is not a newly-coined phrase but a direct extraction of language FGR_019 and its own candidate package already use, independently, in three separate places, to describe this exact subject, and it also mirrors the statute's own self-description ("transparency obligations"). Using the corpus's own already-settled vocabulary, rather than a new synonym, reduces the number of near-duplicate terms a future reader has to reconcile. #1 (`ai_content_disclosure`) is recorded as an equally defensible, immediately-available fallback if a future PM reviewer prefers "disclosure" as the more concrete/operative term over "transparency" as the more abstract/regulatory-framing term — both satisfy every requirement in this addendum identically; the choice between them is a naming preference, not a substantive scoping difference. #3 is not recommended as the primary identifier (see the fit column above) but remains available as evidence that a labeling-centric alternative was genuinely considered, not overlooked.

**Exact semantic scope:** governed knowledge about whether, and under what conditions, content that is AI-generated or AI-manipulated must be disclosed, labeled, or marked as such — to a human audience, a downstream system, or a regulator — under any applicable law. Distinct from: whether the content may be used commercially (`commercial_use`); who owns copyright in AI-generated output (`copyright_ownership`/`copyrightability`); whether a specific real person's likeness/voice/name is used with consent (`likeness`); whether third-party source material was properly licensed (`third_party_source_rights`).

**Claims that would fit:** `CAND-EUAI-ART50-4-AUDIOVISUAL-DEEPFAKE-DISCLOSURE-001` (this candidate — Art. 50(4), first subparagraph, deployer audiovisual disclosure); a future Article 50(2) claim (provider machine-readable-marking obligation — see §6); a future Article 50(4) second-subparagraph claim (deployer public-interest-text disclosure — see §7); a future non-EU jurisdiction's AI-content-labeling statute, should one be governed later.

**Claims that would NOT fit:** any claim whose subject is content-authenticity/provenance marking *not specifically tied to AI origin* (e.g., a hypothetical general "this file was edited" provenance requirement with no AI-generation trigger) — this is a deliberate exclusion, not an oversight, made to keep the topic from collapsing SI8's actual moat (AI-specific judgment) into a broader, less-differentiated "content authenticity" bucket a future engineering/product decision might reasonably want to keep separate; any likeness/consent-specific claim (including the *already-governed* `CLAIM-NY-SYNTHETIC-PERFORMER-DISCLOSURE-001-v1`, which correctly stays under `likeness` — its subject is a real person's replicated identity, not general AI-content-origin disclosure, and this addendum does not propose moving it); any copyright or third-party-source-rights claim.

## 4. Genericity test (per this milestone's own Step 5)

| Case | Result |
|---|---|
| A. Another AI-generated-content disclosure rule (e.g. a future US state AI-labeling statute) | **Fits.** Same subject, different jurisdiction — exactly the reuse case this naming is designed for. |
| B. A non-AI regulatory transparency obligation (e.g. a generic consumer-disclosure law unrelated to AI) | **Correctly excluded.** The name's "ai_content" anchor keeps this out; forcing it in would require a materially different justification this addendum does not find. |
| C. A content-authenticity disclosure rule not specifically about AI origin | **Correctly excluded** (see §3's "claims that would NOT fit"). A content-authenticity rule that IS specifically about AI-generation status (e.g. a hypothetical "must carry AI-generation provenance metadata" requirement) would fit; a general, AI-agnostic authenticity scheme would not. |
| D. A machine-readable marking requirement like Article 50(2) | **Fits** — see §6. |
| E. Public-interest text disclosure (Article 50(4), second subparagraph) | **Fits** — see §7. |

**Result: appropriately scoped.** Neither too narrow (does not hard-code "audiovisual," "deepfake," "Article 50(4)," or "EU" into the identifier — all of B/D/E above would have failed a narrower name) nor too broad (Case B and the non-AI half of Case C are both correctly excluded, preventing the "collapses unrelated future obligations" failure mode this milestone's own Step 3 warns against).

## 5. Article 50(2) boundary (this milestone's Step 6)

**Decision: Article 50(2) belongs under the SAME `KnowledgeTopic` (`ai_content_transparency`), as a SEPARATE future claim — never bundled into `CAND-EUAI-ART50-4-AUDIOVISUAL-DEEPFAKE-DISCLOSURE-001` itself.** This is consistent with, and does not reopen, FGR_019 §2's and the candidate package's own §13 determination that Article 50(2) is "outside this package entirely" at the *claim* level (different actor — provider, not deployer; different mechanism — machine-readable technical marking, not audience-facing disclosure; different Digital Omnibus treatment). That determination concerns claim bundling, not topic identity, and remains fully correct and unaffected by this addendum. What this addendum adds is the topic-level observation §13 did not need to make: a future, independently-drafted Article 50(2) candidate claim would be a legitimate, well-evidenced future member of the *same* `ai_content_transparency` topic, precisely because both paragraphs answer the same general question ("does this AI-generated content need to be identifiable as such to someone") even though they answer it via different actors and mechanisms — the same relationship multiple existing claims under one topic already model (e.g. the several Kling/Pika/Runway commercial-use claims sharing `commercial_use`). No Article 50(2) claim is drafted, proposed in detail, or brought any closer to adoption by this observation.

## 6. Public-interest text boundary (this milestone's Step 7)

**Decision: fits the same `KnowledgeTopic` but remains a separate claim, per the first option this milestone's Step 7 lists.** FGR_019 §2 and the candidate package's own §14 already independently confirm the deferred Article 50(4) second subparagraph (public-interest text) is textually and structurally severable from the first subparagraph this candidate addresses (different content type — text vs. audiovisual; different qualifying test; a materially different exception set, including a human-review/editorial-responsibility exception absent from the first subparagraph entirely). This addendum does not reopen that separation and does not draft the second-subparagraph claim. **Onboarding of the public-interest-text branch remains deferred, exactly as before this addendum** — nothing here changes its status.

## 7. `commercial_use → ai_content_transparency` relationship reassessment (this milestone's Step 8)

**Reassessed, not re-derived from scratch: FGR_019 §9's own independent justification remains substantively sound and is unaffected by which exact `KnowledgeTopic` identifier is ultimately chosen.** FGR_019 §9 tested (per its own account, independently of precedent alone) whether Article 50(4) knowledge genuinely helps interpret a `commercial_use` goal, and found yes: a user asking "can I use this AI video commercially" is asking a question whose complete, honest answer legitimately includes a separate EU disclosure obligation for deep-fake content — the exact shape `TOPIC-RELATIONSHIPS.md`'s own definition requires, and FGR_019 confirms no other existing `GoalCategory` (`likeness`, `copyright_ownership`, `copyrightability`, `third_party_source_rights`) has comparable evidence for this role. Nothing in that reasoning depends on the target topic's own name — it depends only on the target topic's *subject* (AI content transparency), which this addendum's recommendation directly names. **This remains the planned activation path.** No production `TopicRelationship` is authored by this addendum — that remains, as FGR_019 §9 itself states, a future, separately-authorized governance step, contingent on a PM concurrence with the topic identifier recommended here.

## 8. CRC-eligibility consequence (this milestone's Step 9)

**Distinguishing three separate things, per this milestone's own explicit instruction, using FGR_019 exactly:**

FGR_019 §13's representation-issue table classifies the topic-taxonomy gap as **"3 — BLOCKS FINAL GOVERNANCE ADOPTION"** specifically (a required `topic` field cannot be populated without a value), while classifying the explicit-goal `TopicRelationship` (§9), Article 2 applicability representation (§6), and geographic canonicalization (§7) each as **"4 — BLOCKS CRC ACTIVATION ONLY"** — a materially different, and explicitly *not* Adoption-blocking, category. FGR_019 §10 states this directly: "'suitable in principle' is not the same as 'ready for a `crc_eligible: Yes` decision,' which requires a real `topic` assignment this review cannot make."

Therefore:
- **Topic assignment (this addendum's recommendation, once PM-concurred) resolves the classification-3 blocker and enables a PM ADOPTION decision** (`Lifecycle: Candidate → Adopted`) — this is the ONE thing a topic identifier by itself unblocks.
- **Topic assignment does NOT by itself resolve `crc_eligible: Pending → Yes`.** That requires, separately: (a) authoring and PM-adopting the `commercial_use → ai_content_transparency` `TopicRelationship` (§7 above), and (b) a further CRC Publication Review (CPR)-style decision — mirroring this corpus's own established precedent that Adoption and CRC-eligibility are independently-timed acts (e.g. `REL-COPY-OWNERSHIP-COPYRIGHTABILITY-v1` was Adopted 2026-08-16 but only became `crc_eligible: Yes` via a separate CPR on 2026-08-19; `CLAIM-LIKENESS-NY-CONSENT-REQUIREMENT-001-v1` was Adopted with **no** relationship authored at all, `crc_eligible` remaining `Pending` indefinitely without blocking its own Adoption).
- **Topic assignment therefore removes exactly one blocker** (the Adoption-level one) — the Article 2 applicability, geographic canonicalization, and TopicRelationship-authoring blockers (FGR_019 §13's classification-4 items) remain open, unaffected by this addendum, and continue to block CRC activation specifically, not Adoption.
