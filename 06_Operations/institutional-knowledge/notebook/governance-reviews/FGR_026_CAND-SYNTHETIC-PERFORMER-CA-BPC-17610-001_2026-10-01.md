## 2. Phase 2 — Governed subject

The governed subject is California BPC § 17610's disclosure duty for commercial advertisements that prominently include a statutorily-defined synthetic performer — a non-identifiable, AI-generated figure/voice/representation — together with the statute's own enforcement cross-reference (§§ 17500/17200) and its conditional platform/medium duty (§ 17610(e)).

**Explicitly excluded, with reasons:**

## 3. Phase 3 — Proposition drafting

**Draft governed proposition (not yet PM-approved wording):**

> Under California Business and Professions Code § 17610 (added by SB 1050, Chapter 246, Statutes of 2026), it is unlawful for any person to create and cause to be published, in an advertising medium, an advertisement that prominently includes a synthetic performer, without a clear and conspicuous disclosure that the advertisement includes a synthetic performer. "Advertisement" means any audio, video, or audiovisual message or other representation, disseminated by any means including online platforms, that is intended to induce, or reasonably expected to induce, the purchase of goods or services, as described in Business and Professions Code § 17500. "Synthetic performer" means a digital figure, voice, or representation created in whole or in part using generative artificial intelligence that creates the realistic impression of the audio, audiovisual, or visual performance of a human performer who is NOT recognizable as any identifiable natural person; this proposition does not determine whether any specific depicted figure meets or fails this definition. A synthetic performer is used "prominently" only if it is (a) in the foreground and demonstrating or illustrating the product or service, (b) providing or voicing the on- or off-camera narration or commercial message, or (c) illustrating or reacting to the on- or off-camera narration or commercial message; this proposition does not determine whether any specific project's use meets this threshold. The required disclosure must be "clear and conspicuous" — difficult to miss, easily understandable, and presented so a reasonable consumer would notice, read, and comprehend it, given the medium, format, and context — and must use wording substantially similar to "this performance features a synthetic performer" or "no human performer is depicted"; this proposition does not determine whether any specific disclosure, if any, satisfies this standard. The statute expressly does not restrict or prohibit the creation, distribution, or exhibition of synthetic content generally, and does not regulate an advertisement's expressive or informational content beyond the disclosure duty itself. It does not apply to advertisements for expressive works (motion pictures, television programs, streaming content, documentaries, video games, and similar audiovisual works), provided the synthetic performer's use in the advertisement is consistent with its use in the expressive work, nor to advertisements where the use of generative AI solely involves language translation of a human performer or other accessibility features. Separately, an advertising medium (a broadcast station, platform, streaming service, publisher, or similar distributor reaching consumers in California) must not transmit, distribute, display, air, or otherwise make available a non-compliant advertisement once BOTH (i) a court of competent jurisdiction has issued an order finding the advertisement violates this statute or enjoining its creator, and (ii) the medium has been served with that order and information reasonably sufficient to identify the advertisement — this proposition does not determine whether any specific order or service of process satisfies this test. A violation of § 17610 constitutes a violation of § 17500 and may be enforced under the Unfair Competition Law (Chapter 5, commencing with § 17200); § 17610 itself states no separate, synthetic-performer-specific penalty amount. This proposition does not determine whether any specific project's advertisement, performer, disclosure, exemption claim, platform conduct, or distribution reaches consumers in California or otherwise falls within this statute's scope.

This wording: states every definitional element (advertisement, synthetic performer including its non-identifiability predicate, "prominently," "clear and conspicuous," required wording) as the statute's own language, resolving none of them for any project; names all exceptions (expressive-work, translation/accessibility) without resolving either; states the conditional platform/medium duty's own two-part test without resolving it; states the enforcement cross-reference and the absence of a stand-alone penalty amount; imports no NY-specific "actual knowledge" language, no China/NY real-person-likeness terminology, and no legal-compliance or clearance conclusion anywhere.

### 8.B — `non_identifiable_natural_person` / recognizability (Candidate B)

§ 17610(a)(6)'s own closing clause: "...who is NOT recognizable as any identifiable natural person." **Independently re-tested, not assumed rejected merely because China rejected an analogous fact (FGR_025 §7.1).** This is, if anything, an EVEN SHARPER case for rejection than China's: in China, recognizability was a threshold WITHIN one unified consent-based regime (Art. 1019/1020 applies regardless of the recognizability finding's outcome, just with different stakes). ### 8.C — `prominently_used_confirmed` (Candidate C) — ESPECIAL SCRUTINY, per instruction

§ 17610(a)(5): "'Prominently' means the synthetic performer is any of the following: (A) In the foreground and demonstrating or illustrating the product or service. (B) Providing or voicing the on- or off-camera narration or commercial message. (C) Illustrating or reacting to the on- or off-camera narration or commercial message."

**The case FOR admission:** unlike Trademark's Art. 68 (three separate STATUTORY ITEMS with DIFFERENT consequences/thresholds per item), (a)(5)'s three sub-paragraphs are sub-clauses of ONE single statutory definition, with ONE uniform consequence (satisfying any one of (A)/(B)/(C) meets the SAME "prominently" threshold — no item demands a stricter or looser showing than another, unlike Trademark's identity-vs-mere-similarity variation across items). This superficially resembles the permitted disjunctive-existence pattern (NY's `recognizable_likeness_or_voice_present`-style bundling of several named objects into one gate with one uniform consequence, not independently challenged on bundling grounds by `FGR_008` or any later review; China's own content-presence gate, FGR_025 §7.2(a)).

**The case AGAINST admission, independently found more persuasive on this closer scrutiny:** unlike a pure existence gate ("does X appear at all"), each of (A)/(B)/(C) requires characterizing the performer's FUNCTIONAL/NARRATIVE ROLE within the advertisement's own compositional structure — is this figure demonstrating the product (as opposed to merely appearing near it); is this voice providing narration (as opposed to incidental background audio); is this figure reacting to narration (as opposed to simply being visible while narration plays elsewhere). This is a qualitative, content-interpretive judgment of the SAME GENERAL CHARACTER as the judgments this review (§8.B above) and the China sibling (§7.1, §7.4) already reject as Commercial-Assurance-reserved — not a bare observable fact. Architecturally, `ContentPresenceMention` (the only existing project-state type for this kind of fact) has NO role/function/positional field — only `category`, `real_or_synthetic`, `confidence`, `source_turn`, `source_statement` (independently confirmed, `types/interview-engine.ts` lines 616-625) — so even as a permanently-unresolved evidence-only dependency, this candidate would assert a flat HRR-facing label for a fact this architecture has no representational vocabulary for at any finer grain than the claim-wide string itself, no new vocabulary is invented here (per explicit instruction), and doing so would misrepresent a genuine narrative-characterization question as if it were a single observable antecedent fact of Candidate A's own kind. **REJECT as a dependency.** The full three-part definition is stated verbatim in the proposition (§3), unresolved for any project — the identical treatment this review (§8.G) and China (§7.4) give to other statutorily-defined, multi-factor characterization tests, never silently assumed satisfied or unsatisfied.

**(i), content-purpose:** admitted. A single, commonly-applied content-purpose categorization (does this content function as a commercial-inducement message) — the same KIND of single evaluative method as the NY sibling likeness claim's own `advertising_or_trade_use_confirmed` dependency (admitted, unchallenged in this corpus to date), and the same architecture CRC's own `commercial_use` GoalCategory is already built around generally. **(ii), duty-holder role/conduct:** independently reconsidered and REJECTED, diverging deliberately from the NY sibling's own `advertiser_or_duty_holder_status_confirmed` (admitted at FGR_018, which predates the Sept-18 standard and is recorded there as unexamined historical debt, not re-tested here). The divergence is justified, not merely asserted: NY's own duty-holder class is a COMPLEX, multi-element professional-class test ("a person engaged in the BUSINESS of dealing in any property or service who for any COMMERCIAL PURPOSE produces or creates an advertisement") — several conjunctive legal elements plausibly constituting their own single characterization. Consistency with the reasoning that disqualified Candidate C requires disqualifying this candidate too: both require the SAME KIND of qualitative, multi-factor, Domain-L-type content characterization no project-state field represents at any finer grain. **REJECT as a dependency.** The full (a)(3) definition and the (c) wording-similarity requirement are both stated verbatim in the proposition (§3), unresolved for any project.
