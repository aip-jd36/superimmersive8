## 4. Proposition decomposition

Tested directly, not assumed: does the evidence support one proposition or more than one? Unlike Runway (where the evidenced tiers were uniformly permitted, with only an out-of-scope carve-out for unevidenced Enterprise terms), Pika's evidence establishes two genuinely different substantive answers depending on a resolvable project fact — Free is definitively restricted, paid tiers are definitively permitted, both known, not merely one side being an evidence gap. This is structurally the same shape as Kling's baseline-plus-exception (K1's universal default, K2's Member-conditioned override), not Runway's single-uniform-grant shape: the ToS excerpt itself is worded as a general default ("personal, non-commercial use only") with an explicit carve-out ("excluding periods & features for which your subscription plan permits commercial use") — the same "general-rule-with-exception" textual structure Kling's K1/K2 pair exhibits, not a flat feature-comparison table. **The evidence supports exactly two propositions.** A compound, ungated single-statement alternative (stating both halves together with `applicability_requirements: []`, mirroring Runway's shape) was considered and rejected: unlike Runway's Enterprise carve-out (an evidence gap, not a known-different answer), Pika's Free/paid split is a genuine truth-value difference conditioned on a resolvable fact — exactly the case `applicability_requirements` exists to represent, per the same reasoning that produced Kling's Candidate 2. Collapsing it into one ungated statement would under-model the reality and would also forfeit the ability for Bounded Interpretation to represent "unresolved" as a distinct, informative state from a flat recitation of both possibilities — the same architectural value the Kling decomposition captured.
