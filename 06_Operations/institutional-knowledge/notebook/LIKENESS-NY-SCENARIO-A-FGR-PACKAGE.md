## A. Core consent/liability candidate

### `CAND-LIKENESS-NY-CONSENT-REQUIREMENT-001`
- **Proposition:** Under New York Civil Rights Law §§ 50–51, using a living person's name, portrait, picture, likeness, or voice for advertising purposes or for the purposes of trade, within New York, without that person's prior written consent (or, if a minor, the consent of a parent or guardian), can expose the user to civil liability (an injunction, compensatory damages, and — if the use was knowing — exemplary damages at the jury's discretion) and, under § 50 specifically, is a misdemeanor. New York courts have construed "portrait, picture, likeness" to reach any recognizable likeness of the person, not only an exact photograph or an explicit written name.
- **Source references:**
  - primary: N.Y. Civil Rights Law § 50 (Right of Privacy), official text via `nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/CVR/50`.
  - primary: N.Y. Civil Rights Law § 51 (Action for injunction and for damages), official text via `nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/CVR/51`.
  - corroborating: Justia (`law.justia.com/codes/new-york/cvr/article-5/50/`, `.../51/`), FindLaw (`codes.findlaw.com/ny/civil-rights-law/cvr-sect-50/`, `cvr-sect-51`), NY Public Law (`newyork.public.law/laws/n.y._civil_rights_law_section_50`, `..._51`) — all reproduce the same operative sentence.
  - corroborating (recognizability doctrine): Herrick, "A Historical Retrospective on New York's Right of Privacy Law: 115 Years of New York Court of Appeals Jurisprudence" (`herrick.com`); citing *Loftus v. Greenwich Lithographing Co.*, 192 A.D. 251, 182 N.Y.S. 428 (1st Dep't 1920) for the "recognizable likeness, not just an actual photograph" construction.
- **Source authority/type:** Primary legal/official authority (statute), corroborated by strong secondary legal commentary for the recognizability construction.
- **Evidence tier:** See "Evidence-tier disclosure" above — between Class B and Class C; human verbatim capture recommended before FGR.
- **Source fact (as retrieved):** § 50: *"A person, firm or corporation that uses for advertising purposes, or for the purposes of trade, the name, portrait, picture, likeness, or voice of any living person without having first obtained the written consent of such person, or if a minor of such minor's parent or guardian, is guilty of a misdemeanor."* § 51 (operative clause): *"Any person whose name, portrait, picture, likeness or voice is used within this state for advertising purposes or for the purposes of trade without the written consent first obtained may maintain an equitable action... to prevent and restrain the use thereof; and may also sue and recover damages... and if the defendant shall have knowingly used [it]... the jury, in its discretion, may award exemplary damages."*
- **SI8 interpretation:** [SI8 operational judgment, not itself sourced from the statute] A commercial AI video depicting a recognizable real person should not be represented to a client, buyer, or platform as cleared for commercial/advertising use in New York absent confirmed written consent from that person — this is a documentary fact CRC cannot establish from conversation alone (see Dependencies below).

This is a genuine, evidenced architecture gap (see Final Report §Z), not something this claim's authoring can work around, and it is not fabricated to fit the schema — `value: New York` is authored honestly, even though it exposes the gap.
- **Unresolved project dependencies:**

Not currently modeled anywhere in `StructuredUnderstanding` (confirmed in the prior two diagnostics). Candidate content-presence fact for a future Track A extension — see Final Report §Z.
  - `advertising_or_trade_use_confirmed` — whether the specific use is "advertising purposes or purposes of trade" as the statute and its case law construe that phrase (see Claim B below), as opposed to an excluded newsworthy/expressive use. ## B. Scope-boundary candidate

### `CAND-LIKENESS-NY-EXPRESSIVE-WORK-BOUNDARY-001`
- **Proposition:** New York courts have construed "advertising purposes" and "purposes of trade" under §§ 50–51 narrowly, as reaching solicitation of patronage intended to promote the sale of a commodity or service — not every commercial context. Newsworthy content and expressive works (e.g. films, books, works of art) are generally treated as outside "purposes of trade," including when they are themselves sold for profit, unless the use has no genuine relationship to the underlying work or functions as a disguised advertisement (an "incidental use" doctrine also applies to some brief/incidental appearances).
- **Source references:**
  - secondary (strong legal commentary, used because Tier 1 primary case law was not independently, individually re-verified this session — see limitations below): Herrick, "A Historical Retrospective on New York's Right of Privacy Law" (`herrick.com`); Rothman's Roadmap to the Right of Publicity, New York state page (`rightofpublicityroadmap.com/state_page/new-york`); Cornell LII, "Privacy Rights — Newsworthiness" (`law.cornell.edu/nyctap/comments/i00_0001.htm`); Digital Media Law Project, "New York Right of Publicity Law" (`dmlp.org/legal-guide/new-york-right-publicity-law`).
- **Source authority/type:** Strong secondary legal authority interpreting primary case law. **Not yet independently re-verified against a specific primary case opinion this session** — see Evidence limitations.
- **Evidence tier:** Class D-leaning-C (secondary commentary; explicitly not promoted to primary-equivalent status). This is the weaker of the two candidates in this package and should be treated accordingly.
- **Source fact (as characterized by secondary sources, not independently confirmed against a primary opinion this session):** "use for 'advertising purposes' is defined as solicitation for patronage, intended to promote the sale of some collateral commodity or service"; "New York courts have frequently held that movies, works of art, and magazines are uses that are newsworthy and/or matters of public interest... the state also recognizes an incidental use exception"; "if uses have no relationship to the underlying work or the use is a disguised advertisement, there may be liability even if the use appears in an expressive work."
- **SI8 interpretation:** [SI8 operational judgment] This boundary matters directly for CRC's own honesty: without it, CRC would risk implying §§ 50–51 reaches any commercial-context depiction of a real person, which secondary authority indicates is not correct — some commercially-distributed expressive content falls outside "purposes of trade" entirely. Neither this claim nor Claim A should be read together as resolving where any specific project falls.
- **Applicability requirements:** None authored — this candidate describes a general interpretive doctrine, not a project-conditional rule; encoding it as a formal applicability gate would require a project-state fact ("is this use advertising vs. expressive," itself a legal characterization) this codebase's own discipline (§10 of the diagnostic task, §13 of this task) prohibits fabricating.
- **Unresolved project dependencies:**
  - `advertising_or_trade_use_confirmed` — same dependency as Claim A; this candidate exists specifically to inform how CRC frames that unresolved dependency, not to resolve it.
- **Lifecycle:** Candidate. **CRC eligible:** Pending.
- **Refresh class:** LOW — doctrinal, case-law-derived, slow-moving relative to statutory or provider-policy content.
- **CRC could safely say (bounded, and only together with Claim A, never alone):** New York's advertising/trade-use requirement has been read by courts to exclude some uses even in commercial contexts — for example, newsworthy or expressive content generally, and incidental appearances — so §§ 50–51 does not necessarily apply just because content is used commercially in some general sense.
- **CRC must not conclude:** Whether this project's specific use falls inside or outside "advertising purposes or purposes of trade"; whether any exception (newsworthiness, expressive work, incidental use) actually applies to this project.
