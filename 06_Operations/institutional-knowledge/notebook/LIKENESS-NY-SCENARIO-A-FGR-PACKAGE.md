## A. Core consent/liability candidate

### `CLAIM-LIKENESS-NY-CONSENT-REQUIREMENT-001-v1` (Adopted 2026-08-28 — see below; formerly `CAND-LIKENESS-NY-CONSENT-REQUIREMENT-001`)
- **Proposition (corrected per `FGR_008` §17, applied 2026-08-28 — the recognizability sentence below was removed, not merely reworded; see "Deferred sub-consideration" note below for why):** Under New York Civil Rights Law §§ 50–51, using a living person's name, portrait, picture, likeness, or voice for advertising purposes or for the purposes of trade, within New York, without that person's prior written consent (or, if a minor, the consent of a parent or guardian), can expose the user to civil liability (an injunction, compensatory damages, and — if the use was knowing — exemplary damages at the jury's discretion) and, under § 50 specifically, is a misdemeanor.
- **Deferred sub-consideration, not yet supported (removed from the governed proposition 2026-08-28, per `FGR_008` §4/§17 — preserved here, not deleted, for a possible future separately-evidenced sub-candidate):** New York courts have been characterized by secondary commentary (Herrick, citing *Loftus v. ## B. Scope-boundary candidate

### `CAND-LIKENESS-NY-EXPRESSIVE-WORK-BOUNDARY-001`
- **Proposition:** New York courts have construed "advertising purposes" and "purposes of trade" under §§ 50–51 narrowly, as reaching solicitation of patronage intended to promote the sale of a commodity or service — not every commercial context. Newsworthy content and expressive works (e.g. films, books, works of art) are generally treated as outside "purposes of trade," including when they are themselves sold for profit, unless the use has no genuine relationship to the underlying work or functions as a disguised advertisement (an "incidental use" doctrine also applies to some brief/incidental appearances).
- **Source references:**
  - secondary (strong legal commentary, used because Tier 1 primary case law was not independently, individually re-verified this session — see limitations below): Herrick, "A Historical Retrospective on New York's Right of Privacy Law" (`herrick.com`); Rothman's Roadmap to the Right of Publicity, New York state page (`rightofpublicityroadmap.com/state_page/new-york`); Cornell LII, "Privacy Rights — Newsworthiness" (`law.cornell.edu/nyctap/comments/i00_0001.htm`); Digital Media Law Project, "New York Right of Publicity Law" (`dmlp.org/legal-guide/new-york-right-publicity-law`).
- **Source authority/type:** Strong secondary legal authority interpreting primary case law, now partially corroborated by a genuine (non-LLM) primary-database excerpt (2026-08-27) — see below. **The complete primary opinion was not obtained** — see Evidence limitations.
- **Evidence tier:** Class D-leaning-C (secondary commentary), with one upgrade: *Stephano v. News Group Publications, Inc.*, 64 N.Y.2d 174 (N.Y. 1984) — the closest identified controlling authority for this candidate's doctrine — was located via CourtListener's public case-law database and confirmed real (correct citation, court, judge Wachtler, decided 1984-12-20), with a genuine verbatim opening excerpt directly from the opinion: *"The plaintiff, a professional model, claims that the defendant used his picture for trade or advertising purposes without his consent, and thus violated his statutory right to privacy (Civil Rights Law, § 51), by publishing a picture of him modeling a 'bomber jacket' in a magazine article containing information regarding the approximate price of the jacket, the name of the d[esigner...]"* (excerpt truncated by the source database, not by this session). This confirms the case exists, is correctly cited, and is genuinely on-topic — it does **not** independently confirm the specific "solicitation for patronage"/newsworthiness holding language this candidate's proposition rests on, which remains secondary-sourced only. **Recorded as PRIMARY CASE EVIDENCE INCOMPLETE**, not silently treated as verified. This is still the weaker of the two candidates in this package and should be treated accordingly.
- **Source fact (as characterized by secondary sources, not independently confirmed against a primary opinion this session):** "use for 'advertising purposes' is defined as solicitation for patronage, intended to promote the sale of some collateral commodity or service"; "New York courts have frequently held that movies, works of art, and magazines are uses that are newsworthy and/or matters of public interest... the state also recognizes an incidental use exception"; "if uses have no relationship to the underlying work or the use is a disguised advertisement, there may be liability even if the use appears in an expressive work."
- **SI8 interpretation:** [SI8 operational judgment] This boundary matters directly for CRC's own honesty: without it, CRC would risk implying §§ 50–51 reaches any commercial-context depiction of a real person, which secondary authority indicates is not correct — some commercially-distributed expressive content falls outside "purposes of trade" entirely. Neither this claim nor Claim A should be read together as resolving where any specific project falls.
- **Applicability requirements:** None authored — this candidate describes a general interpretive doctrine, not a project-conditional rule; encoding it as a formal applicability gate would require a project-state fact ("is this use advertising vs. expressive," itself a legal characterization) this codebase's own discipline (§10 of the diagnostic task, §13 of this task) prohibits fabricating.
- **Unresolved project dependencies:**
  - `advertising_or_trade_use_confirmed` — same dependency as Claim A; this candidate exists specifically to inform how CRC frames that unresolved dependency, not to resolve it.
- **Lifecycle:** Candidate. **CRC eligible:** Pending.
- **Refresh class:** LOW — doctrinal, case-law-derived, slow-moving relative to statutory or provider-policy content.
- **CRC could safely say (bounded, and only together with Claim A, never alone):** New York's advertising/trade-use requirement has been read by courts to exclude some uses even in commercial contexts — for example, newsworthy or expressive content generally, and incidental appearances — so §§ 50–51 does not necessarily apply just because content is used commercially in some general sense.
- **CRC must not conclude:** Whether this project's specific use falls inside or outside "advertising purposes or purposes of trade"; whether any exception (newsworthiness, expressive work, incidental use) actually applies to this project.

## Complete § 51 exceptions (verbatim-confirmed 2026-08-27, classified against Scenario A)

| Exception | Verbatim substance | Classification |
|---|---|---|
