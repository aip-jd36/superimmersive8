# SI8 Buyer Analysis — Market Driver Framework

**Created:** June 6, 2026
**Analyst:** JD Chang + Claude Sonnet 4.6
**Purpose:** For each market driver, answer 7 questions critical to product development and sales strategy. Maps to warm lead signals in `03_Sales/SALES-PIPELINE.md`.

---

## Framework Questions

For each market driver:
- **a) Who is the buyer?** — Title, company type, decision-making authority
- **b) How are they currently solving it?** — The status quo SI8 is replacing
- **c) Why choose SI8 over the current solution?** — The switching rationale
- **d) What creates more urgency?** — Internal/external factors that make the problem more severe
- **e) What does the buyer need to believe?** — Mental prerequisites for purchase
- **f) What is the total cost of their current solution?** — Time + money + risk (basis for ROI)
- **g) One-time or recurring purchase?** — Drives product design and pricing strategy

---

## Additional PM/Discovery Questions

Beyond the 7 above, the following questions are critical for product development and should be asked in discovery calls:

- **h) What does the decision-making process look like?** — Solo (boutique founder) vs. committee (holdco). Who else is involved? What approvals are needed? Shapes sales cycle length.
- **i) What does "good enough" look like to the buyer?** — Is the bar low enough that a simpler product satisfies them? This determines minimum viable product vs. premium tier boundary.
- **j) What causes a buyer to NOT buy after expressing interest?** — The specific objections that kill deals (price, credibility, turnaround time, "we'll do it ourselves"). Highest value question for conversion optimization.
- **k) What does the buyer tell themselves the problem costs them?** — Buyers systematically underestimate the cost of inaction. Their mental model of the cost must be above $499 for them to buy. Understanding the gap helps frame the problem, not just the solution.
- **l) Who else inside their organization benefits from this being solved?** — Champion (wants it solved) vs. decision-maker (approves budget) vs. beneficiary (whose life gets easier). These can be three different people. Knowing all three speeds the sale.
- **m) At what point do they bring this in-house?** — When does hiring someone make more sense than continuing to use SI8? This reveals the ceiling on the relationship and informs enterprise pricing.
- **n) Are there adjacent vendors the buyer already uses who could add this feature?** — Legal tech platforms, agency operating systems, IP management tools. These are the competitive threats to watch — not startups building the same thing, but incumbents who could bundle it.

## Driver 1: Campaign Blocked by Brand Legal Team

*The most immediate, most commercial, highest-purchase-urgency driver. Campaigns are being blocked today.*

### a) Who is the buyer?

The **agency** — specifically whoever is accountable for campaign delivery:

| Agency Type | Likely Buyer Title | Decision Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Boutique / indie (1-20 people) | Founder, CD, or MD | Solo — buys same day |
| Mid-size production / content agency | Head of Production or Creative Technology Lead | 1-2 approvals needed |
| Holdco agency arm | Gen AI Specialist, Senior CD | Requires internal sign-off — longer cycle |

The **brand legal team** creates the problem but does not buy the solution. The brand's legal team is the gatekeeper; the agency is the payor.

Key characteristic: the buyer is producing AI video **for clients**, not for themselves. The pain is commercial — their campaign is being blocked — not intellectual.

### b) How are they currently solving it?

Most agencies are improvising. The current state is a patchwork:
- Writing informal emails summarizing what tools they used ("We used Runway for the main sequence, Pika for the transitions")
- Sharing raw prompt logs directly with the client's legal contact
- Some have built internal "approved tools" lists they share with clients
- Some repeat verbally "we checked and it's fine"
- Some are losing campaigns without knowing documentation was the issue
- Some pay their own entertainment lawyer to write a letter for high-value campaigns
- The most sophisticated (Gabriel Preston, Imagine This Creative Studio) have built their own informal system: approved platform lists, prompt sheets, workflow docs — but it took significant time to build and is not standardized

The defining characteristic of the current state: **a different improvised solution for every client**, no standardized output, no audit trail.

### c) Why choose SI8 over the current solution?

- **Third-party credibility**: "SI8 reviewed it" carries more weight with a brand's legal team than "we checked." The legal team is not reassured by the party creating the risk certifying its own work. An independent verifier changes the dynamic.
- **Covers what agencies miss**: SI8's review covers training data licensing disclosure, likeness rights, music copyright, brand safety — categories agencies typically don't include in their ad-hoc emails
- **Professional document format**: A branded Chain of Title PDF signals seriousness vs. a loose email trail
- **Speed**: 90-minute turnaround is faster than waiting for their own lawyer to respond
- **Cost**: $499 to unblock a $20K-$100K campaign is an obvious ROI calculation
- **SI8 Certified badge differentiates in pitches**: "We have a documented AI compliance process" is a competitive differentiator when brands are comparing agencies
- **Risk transfer**: SI8's review documentation means the agency isn't the only party who assessed the risk — SI8 holds a record of the review

### d) What creates more urgency?

Ranked roughly by immediacy:
1. **A campaign that actually got blocked** — personal experience is the strongest trigger. Until it happens to them, many agencies underestimate the risk.
2. **A competitor winning a pitch by showing compliance documentation** — "the other agency had documentation and we didn't" creates immediate action
3. **A brand RFP that explicitly requires AI documentation capabilities** — once one major brand starts requiring this, others follow
4. **A specific regulated-sector client joining their roster** — finance, pharma, healthcare clients bring stricter requirements
5. **A holding group mandate with a deadline** — "all AI campaigns require documentation by Q3" from the network
6. **August 2 EU AI Act enforcement** — makes brand legal teams at EU-facing companies raise their documentation bar

### e) What does the buyer need to believe?

- **SI8's document will actually satisfy their client's legal team** — not just generate more paper. The biggest objection: "will this actually work?"
- **The review is substantive** — that 90 minutes of human review is meaningful, not a rubber stamp
- **$499 is worth it relative to DIY** — they need to believe their own solution is inadequate or too costly
- **SI8 doesn't create liability for them** — a bad certification that gets challenged could make the agency's situation worse, not better. The buyer needs to understand the scope and limitations.

### f) Total cost of current solution?

| Cost Component | Estimate |
|---|---|
| Time drafting an ad-hoc email/doc per campaign | 2-4 hrs of CD or senior PM time |
| Entertainment lawyer for high-value campaigns | $500–$2,000 per engagement |
| Building an internal process (Gabriel Preston route) | 20-40+ hrs of executive time, ongoing maintenance |
| Lost campaign if blocked | Thousands in revenue + client relationship risk |
| Brand liability if something goes wrong (no paper trail) | Variable — potentially very high |

**The key number for selling**: "If your senior CD spends 3 hours on this per campaign at a $150/hr blended rate, that's $450 of time — plus legal exposure. $499 for a defensible third-party review is break-even on time alone."

### g) One-time or recurring?

**Recurring, per campaign.** This is the most important structural insight for Driver 1.

Every new AI video campaign needs its own Chain of Title. An active boutique agency producing 2-4 AI campaigns per month generates 2-4 submissions per month. This is a subscription in disguise.

Natural pricing evolution:
- **Today**: $499 per video (converts the first buyer)
- **Month 3-6**: Volume bundles: 5 verifications for $1,999 / 10 for $3,500 / monthly retainer
- **Year 2**: Agency subscription tier with unlimited reviews up to X per month

## Driver 2: Holding Group IP Policy / Client Contract Requirement

*Longer sales cycle, higher deal value, enterprise potential.*

### a) Who is the buyer?

Two distinct buyer profiles with very different sales motions:

**Profile 2A — Agency Inside a Holdco Network (the immediate buyer)**
- A Creative Director, Head of Production, or Gen AI Specialist at an agency within WPP/Publicis/IPG/Omnicom/Havas
- Needs to comply with their network's AI policy and demonstrate compliance to their holding group and clients
- Can often buy at $499/campaign on a company card
- The Gen AI Specialist who champions SI8 internally becomes the person who "solved AI compliance" for their division — career advancement incentive

**Profile 2B — Holding Group Procurement (the enterprise sale)**
- A compliance officer, CDO, or procurement lead at the holding group level
- Procuring a solution for their entire agency network
- This is a 3-6 month sales cycle at potentially $50K-$200K+ ARR contract value
- Requires a different pitch, a proof-of-concept deployment, and executive-level engagement

### b) How are they currently solving it?

- **"Approved AI tools" lists**: WPP, Publicis, IPG have published lists of approved AI tools (largely limiting to Adobe Firefly, Getty-licensed tools) — this solves the sourcing problem but not the documentation problem
- **Internal checklists and self-certification**: Agencies within the network complete a form saying they've complied — no third-party review
- **Legal review for high-profile campaigns only**: Full legal review is reserved for campaigns with high brand visibility or regulated clients; most campaigns slip through without review
- **Doing nothing and hoping**: The majority of agencies in holdco networks have not implemented any systematic AI documentation. The approved tools list provides cover but not proof.

### c) Why choose SI8 over the current solution?

- **Standardized output across the network**: A holding group can point to "our agencies use SI8 Certified documentation for all AI campaigns" rather than managing 20 different internal processes
- **Third-party review is more defensible**: When a client's procurement team asks "how do you ensure AI compliance?", a named third-party verifier is a stronger answer than "we have an internal checklist"
- **Cheaper and faster than legal review at scale**: At $499/campaign vs. $1,500-3,000 for a lawyer, and 90 minutes vs. 2-4 weeks, the economics work at volume
- **The Gen AI Specialist's career case**: Internal champion becomes the person who introduced a scalable solution to a real problem

### d) What creates more urgency?

1. **A brand client explicitly requires SI8-level documentation in their MSA** — when one major brand (P&G, Unilever, Ford) adds AI documentation to their supplier requirements, the holding group must respond
2. **A holding group deadline**: "all AI campaigns must be documented by Q3 2026" creates immediate action across the network
3. **A publicized incident**: a competitor holdco agency caught in an AI IP dispute, the holding group CEO reads about it and asks the question downward
4. **A regulated-sector account**: a pharma or financial services client joining the network brings strict compliance requirements
5. **A competitor agency winning a pitch** by demonstrating documented AI compliance

### e) What does the buyer need to believe?

- **SI8 is credible enough for their holding group's compliance team** — not just a startup with a PDF generator. "Who reviews it?" is the first question.
- **The process is scalable to their volume** — if they run 50 AI campaigns per month, is 90-minute review per campaign viable?
- **SI8's review is legally defensible** — particularly who the reviewers are and what qualifications they have
- **Integration is low-friction** — they won't want a complex onboarding process

### f) Total cost of current solution?

| Cost Component | Estimate |
|---|---|
| Legal review for high-value campaigns | $1,500–$3,000 per engagement × N campaigns |
| Internal compliance staff (dedicated AI compliance role) | $80K–$150K/yr salary |
| Building an internal framework | $20K–$50K legal/consulting engagement |
| Losing a brand account due to non-compliance | Potentially millions |
| Brand reputational risk from an AI IP incident | Immeasurable |

### g) One-time or recurring?

**Recurring — and the natural structure is a volume contract, not per-campaign.**

For a holdco-level buyer, per-campaign pricing doesn't scale. The commercial model should be:
- A monthly or annual contract for X reviews across all network agencies
- Per-agency seat pricing (each agency pays a monthly fee for access)
- Volume discounts that make per-review cost drop significantly at scale

This is the enterprise sale: sign one holding group, activate 20+ agencies.

## Driver 3: E&O Insurance for Productions

*Different buyer type — film/production, not advertising agency. Lower volume, higher per-engagement value.*

### a) Who is the buyer?

- **Independent or boutique production company** producing content that needs E&O for distribution (films, series, documentaries, branded entertainment)
- **Line producer or executive producer** — the person responsible for clearing the production for distribution
- **Branded content producer at an agency** — agency that produces long-form content for a brand, not just ads
- **Indirect buyer: E&O underwriter** — if underwriters start requiring SI8 documentation as a condition of issuing AI-content policies, they effectively mandate SI8 even though they don't pay for it

Key distinction from Drivers 1-2: this buyer is in **film/TV production**, not advertising. The pain is different — not "my campaign is blocked" but "my production can't get distribution."

### b) How are they currently solving it?

- **Traditional entertainment lawyers**: Chain of title review at $1,000-$5,000 per engagement, 2-4 week turnaround — this is the established market SI8 can displace
- **Sticking to safe tools**: Using Adobe Firefly, Getty-licensed tools, or content with clear IP indemnification policies to avoid the question
- **Avoiding AI entirely**: Some productions that need E&O are simply not using AI to avoid the documentation problem
- **Self-certifying to the underwriter**: Writing a memo stating they've taken reasonable precautions — effectiveness varies by underwriter

### c) Why choose SI8 over the current solution?

- **Faster than a traditional entertainment lawyer**: 90 minutes vs. weeks — critical when productions are on deadline
- **Cheaper than a lawyer for the same scope**: $499 vs. $1,500-$5,000
- **AI-specific expertise**: Traditional entertainment lawyers are not fluent in AI tool licensing, training data disclosure, or AI-specific risk categories — SI8 is
- **Third-party verification**: Adds credibility over self-certification for the underwriter
- **Standardized output**: If underwriters begin to recognize SI8's format as a credible input to AI content coverage decisions, it becomes the industry standard for this problem

### d) What creates more urgency?

1. **A production that couldn't get E&O coverage due to AI content** — the production stalls, the distribution deal is in jeopardy
2. **An underwriter explicitly asking for AI provenance documentation** as a condition of issuing a policy
3. **A major streaming platform adding AI documentation requirements** to their content delivery specifications (Netflix, Amazon, Apple TV+)
4. **Growth in AI-heavy productions**: As more productions use AI tools more extensively, the E&O question becomes unavoidable rather than occasional

### e) What does the buyer need to believe?

- **SI8's documentation will satisfy the specific underwriter's requirements** — this varies by underwriter and is the key uncertainty
- **SI8's review scope is comparable to what an entertainment lawyer would produce** — the underwriter may have a format preference
- **The 90-minute turnaround is credible for high-stakes review** — "how thorough can you be in 90 minutes?" is the natural objection

### f) Total cost of current solution?

| Cost Component | Estimate |
|---|---|
| Entertainment lawyer for chain of title review | $1,500–$5,000 per production |
| Time: 2-4 week lawyer turnaround | Production delay + opportunity cost |
| Failed E&O application | Distribution deal may be lost — entire production value at risk |
| Avoiding AI entirely | Significant creative and competitive cost |

**The ROI case**: A production company that gets one deal past the E&O gate with SI8's documentation has paid for a year of SI8 use with that single transaction.

### g) One-time or recurring?

**Per-production, lower frequency.** A production company making 2-4 productions per year is a 2-4x/year buyer. Lower cadence than Drivers 1-2, but higher stakes per engagement.

A natural bundled product: "SI8 Production Pack" — covers all AI-generated elements across a single production for a flat fee higher than $499, more appropriate for a production with full E&O risk.

## Driver 4: Platform Content Policies (YouTube, Meta, Google Ads)

*The weakest standalone driver for SI8. Platform labeling is addressed by platform's own tools. SI8 is an audit trail, not the label itself.*

### a) Who is the buyer?

- Agency or brand **social media manager** or **performance marketing lead** — the person who actually publishes the ad
- **Agency compliance / operations** lead who wants a policy for consistent labeling across all campaigns
- NOT a strong standalone buyer — this is a supporting benefit of the main product

### b) How are they currently solving it?

- Using the **platform's own disclosure tools** at point of posting (Meta's "made with AI" toggle, YouTube's disclosure checkbox, TikTok's label system)
- Social media managers handle it case-by-case — not systematic
- No documentation retained beyond the platform flag

### c) Why choose SI8 over the current solution?

- SI8 does **not** replace platform labeling — the platform's own tools handle that
- SI8's value for this driver is **downstream audit trail**: if an ad is flagged or pulled, SI8 documentation is the evidence that due diligence was performed before the campaign ran
- For brands that want comprehensive documentation across all channels, SI8 gives one document vs. platform-by-platform disclosure
- **Honest assessment**: This is a marginal value-add for most buyers, not a purchase driver on its own

### d) What creates more urgency?

1. A campaign getting pulled mid-flight on Meta or YouTube — this is the first time most brands feel real pain around this
2. Platform enforcement becoming more consistent and aggressive
3. A high-profile brand incident: AI content exposed as unlabeled, goes viral negatively

### e) What does the buyer need to believe?

- That having a pre-campaign audit trail actually matters when a platform flags content
- That the platform won't just accept their own disclosure toggle as sufficient

### f) Total cost of current solution?

Minimal — the platform disclosure tools are free. The cost is only felt when a campaign is pulled (lost media spend, creative waste, brand risk).

### g) One-time or recurring?

Recurring per campaign if used for this purpose — but again, SI8 should not be sold primarily on this basis.

**Verdict**: Do not lead with this driver. It's a supporting benefit to name when the buyer asks "what else does this protect me from?" — not a primary sales hook.

## Driver 5: IAB AI Transparency Framework (Voluntary Industry Guidelines)

*Longer horizon. Most valuable for reaching compliance-minded buyers at larger agencies. Weakest enforcement teeth today.*

### a) Who is the buyer?

- **Head of Compliance, General Counsel, or CDO** at a mid-to-large agency
- **Brand marketing procurement officer** building AI supplier requirements
- An agency that wants to be on **preferred/certified supplier lists** for IAB-compliant brands
- This buyer is more senior and process-oriented than the Driver 1 buyer — they're thinking about risk management, not just today's blocked campaign

### b) How are they currently solving it?

- Building internal compliance checklists based on the IAB framework
- Hiring a "Responsible AI" or "AI Ethics" lead (larger agencies only)
- Joining IAB working groups to help shape standards
- Most honestly: **doing nothing yet** — the framework is 6 months old and most agencies have not implemented anything

### c) Why choose SI8 over the current solution?

- "IAB says: assess and document. We're the operational implementation of that framework."
- Faster and cheaper to deploy than building an internal process
- Signals to brand clients that the agency has taken IAB seriously with a third-party review
- SI8 Certified = tangible evidence of IAB-aligned assessment

### d) What creates more urgency?

1. A brand RFP that explicitly asks "Do you have an IAB AI compliance documentation capability?"
2. A brand threatening to remove non-compliant agencies from their preferred supplier list
3. IAB guidelines becoming incorporated into holding group supplier requirements (this is coming)
4. ASA enforcement actions referencing IAB standards in rulings (this would give voluntary guidelines real teeth)

### e) What does the buyer need to believe?

- That IAB guidelines will eventually have real consequences — either through holding group mandates or regulatory incorporation
- That being ahead of the compliance curve is worth paying for now vs. scrambling later
- That SI8's output is recognizable as IAB-aligned assessment (not just SI8's own standard)

### f) Total cost of current solution?

| Cost Component | Estimate |
|---|---|
| Hiring an internal AI compliance lead | $80K–$150K/yr |
| External counsel to build an internal framework | $20K–$50K engagement |
| Ongoing compliance monitoring | 0.5-1 FTE ongoing |

SI8 at $499/campaign is dramatically cheaper as a per-engagement solution for an agency that hasn't yet scaled to needing dedicated compliance staff.

### g) One-time or recurring?

Recurring per campaign — same logic as Driver 1. This buyer may be interested in an agency-level retainer once they're convinced of SI8's value.

### The Fastest Path to Revenue (Year 1)

Driver 3 (E&O) is an under-served market SI8 hasn't yet reached. The buyer is different (production, not advertising) and requires different positioning.

### Recurring vs. One-Time Summary

Drivers 1, 2, and 5: inherently recurring — every new campaign needs its own Chain of Title. This is the structural basis for a subscription/retainer model at scale.

Driver 3: per-production, lower frequency, potentially higher value per engagement.

Driver 4: marginal SI8 value — do not lead with this.

## Key Takeaways for Product and Sales

1. **Driver 1 is where Year 1 revenue comes from.** Every active warm lead with a Trigger 1 signal maps here. The pitch is simple, the ROI is obvious, the decision is fast. Optimize sales motion for this archetype first.

2. **Driver 2 is the enterprise play.** No Driver 2 conversions yet, but 4 holdco signals in pipeline. The unlock: one successful Driver 1 use case inside a holdco network that gets referenced upward. Matt Sergison-Main (OLIVER/Brandtech) is the most important lead in the pipeline for this reason — OLIVER is inside Brandtech, which is a major holdco.

3. **Driver 3 is an untapped segment.** The E&O buyer is real and has clear ROI, but SI8's current Sales Nav targeting is not reaching them. Reaching production companies (EP, Line Producer, Head of Production) requires different search criteria. Worth a dedicated test once Drivers 1-2 are converting.

4. **The recurring purchase structure is the business model.** Every Driver 1 and 2 buyer needs SI8 per campaign. A boutique agency doing 3 AI campaigns/month is worth $18K/yr in per-campaign revenue, or a natural retainer target. Introduce volume pricing by Q3 2026.

5. **The "upgrade buyer" segment (Trigger 3) is strategically underserved.** Gabriel Preston already has an informal system. Christopher Neitzert says he has a solution. Florent Delavous is building something. These people already KNOW the problem is real — they don't need convincing. The pitch is: "Your informal system isn't SI8 Certified. A brand's legal team won't accept your own spreadsheet the same way they'll accept a third-party audit." This segment converts faster than cold leads because the discovery conversation is already done.
