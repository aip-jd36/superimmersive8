# New York Synthetic Performer Disclosure Law — Research & SI8 Analysis

**Law:** S.8420-A / A.8887-B — amends New York General Business Law § 396-b
**Signed:** December 11, 2025 (Governor Kathy Hochul)
**Effective:** June 9, 2026
**Researched:** June 14, 2026
**Purpose:** Track the law's requirements, legal community guidance, documentation gap, and SI8 positioning implications.

---

## Bottom Line for SI8

The law mandates a **disclosure** ("this ad contains AI-generated performers") but is completely silent on how brands prove they did their due diligence. The "actual knowledge" standard creates a compliance liability that no existing format addresses. **Nobody is calling it chain of title — but that's exactly what they're describing.**

The term "chain of title" does not appear in any law firm guidance reviewed as of June 14, 2026. The concept does appear — as "vendor chain documentation," "traceability to creation source," "provenance of the underlying training data" — but the field is naming the problem without naming the solution. SI8 is the solution that doesn't exist yet in public guidance.

## 1. What the Law Actually Says

### Definition: "Synthetic Performer"
> "A digitally created asset created, reproduced, or modified by computer, using generative artificial intelligence or a software algorithm, that is intended to create the impression that the asset is engaging in an audiovisual and/or visual performance of a human performer who is not recognizable as any identifiable natural performer."

Key implication: this covers AI-generated human figures who are **not real people** (i.e., not a likeness/deepfake of a specific person). A fully generated AI spokesperson in an ad is within scope. A real person whose likeness was replicated is covered under a separate statute (right of publicity).

### Who Is Liable
Liability attaches to the **advertiser** — not the production company, not the AI tool vendor, not the media platform. Platforms that host or distribute ads are expressly exempt.

This means: a brand cannot outsource its compliance risk. If the agency used a synthetic performer and didn't tell them, the brand is still liable.

### The Disclosure Requirement
Advertisers must make a **"conspicuous disclosure"** in the advertisement itself when a synthetic performer is used, provided the advertiser has "actual knowledge" of its inclusion.

What counts as conspicuous: **undefined in the statute.** No format, placement, language, or timing requirements specified.

### Exemptions
- Audio-only advertisements
- Language translation of a human performer
- Expressive works (films, TV, streaming, documentaries, video games) where the synthetic performer's use in the ad is consistent with its use in the underlying work

### Penalties
- First violation: **$1,000**
- Subsequent violations: **$5,000 per violation**

## 2. The "Actual Knowledge" Problem

This is the live compliance gap — and SI8's entry point.

The statute requires disclosure only when the advertiser has "actual knowledge" that a synthetic performer appears. This creates a two-sided problem:

**Side A — Brands who don't know:** If an agency delivers AI-generated video and doesn't flag the synthetic performer, is the brand liable? Legal consensus: yes, if they were "willfully blind" — deliberately avoiding information they should reasonably have. Brands cannot simply not ask.

**Side B — Brands who do know:** How do they document that they knew and that they reviewed it? The statute provides **zero guidance** on what constitutes adequate due diligence.

Every law firm reviewed (Crowell & Moring, Manatt, Cooley, Skadden, McDermott, DWT) flags this as the enforcement ambiguity. None provide a documentation format for resolving it.

**The gap SI8 fills:** A Chain of Title document creates a paper trail showing what tool was used, whether it generates synthetic performers, whether commercial use is authorized, and that a human reviewer confirmed the assessment. That is what "actual knowledge + due diligence" looks like as a document.

## 3. What Legal Guidance Is Recommending (As of June 2026)

No law firm is recommending a Chain of Title format. They are recommending the *components* of one under different names.

### Documentation language from active legal guidance:

**McDermott Will & Emery:**
> "Maintain records regarding the creation and use of AI-generated advertising content, including vendor disclosures and internal review records relevant to whether the company had actual knowledge that a synthetic performer was included."

**Cooley:**
> "Tightening contracts with agencies, production studios and AI vendors to require upfront identification of synthetic performers, allocate responsibility for disclosures, and address the 'actual knowledge' standard through clear internal escalation and documentation practices."

**Influencers Time (compliance audit guide — most operationally specific):**
> "Can you trace the AI talent back to the tool or vendor that created it, and does that vendor's terms of service include consent warranties for commercial use?"

> "Any AI production tool, creative agency, or content studio you use for ad production should be required to certify, in writing, that synthetic performers in delivered assets comply with New York law."

Required documentation they recommend brands maintain:
1. **Consent documentation** — signed agreement covering commercial advertising use in NY
2. **Disclosure labeling proof** — evidence of compliant placement
3. **Vendor chain documentation** — complete traceability to creation source
4. **Jurisdictional records** — proof the ad targets or runs in NY

**Akerman LLP:**
> "Franchisors with creative, advertising, and influencer contracts need to require agencies and vendors to flag synthetic-performer use, include indemnity for nondisclosure, and maintain material proof of disclosure compliance."

**Manatt:**
> "Evaluate how third-party agencies, production partners or vendors use (or plan to use) generative AI in creating marketing assets and update contractual protection, including representations, warranties and indemnities, as appropriate."

### What's conspicuously absent from all guidance:
- A standardized format for vendor documentation
- Any template for what a "vendor certification" looks like
- Any mention of chain of title as a concept
- Any mention of training data documentation
- Any definition of what proves due diligence in enforcement

This is the format vacuum. The law is live, the requirement exists, and nobody has built the document.

## 4. C2PA / Provenance Metadata: Where It Stands

One source (Influencers Time brand compliance guide) mentions:
> "embedding C2PA provenance metadata in AI-touched assets going forward"

This is the only direct provenance/metadata reference found across all sources reviewed as of June 14, 2026. It is positioned as a **best practice**, not a compliance standard.

**The C2PA gap (consistent with SI8's known positioning):**
- C2PA tells you *what tool* made the video
- C2PA does not tell you whether the TOS permits commercial use
- C2PA does not assess IP risk, likeness presence, or synthetic performer scope
- C2PA is embedded by tool providers (Runway, Kling, Pika) — not always present
- C2PA metadata cannot be "reviewed" — it's machine-readable, not human-certified

SI8's position remains valid: C2PA is metadata infrastructure. SI8 is the judgment layer that interprets it and produces a human-certified document.

## 6. SI8 Outreach Implications

### What works as a hook:
The NY Synthetic Performer Law creates a **liability that attaches to brands** for campaigns where they had "actual knowledge." The compliance documentation gap — what does that knowledge look like on paper? — is where SI8 enters.

**For legal team outreach (current NY 1st Law campaign):**
> "The NY law doesn't specify what documentation brands need to prove they did the review. That format is being defined right now. We've built it. [link to sample]"

**What to be careful about:**
The law is primarily a **disclosure law** (did the ad label the AI?) — not a provenance documentation law (can you prove where the AI came from?). SI8 answers the second question. The first is simpler (just put a label in the ad). Don't oversell SI8 as the disclosure solution — it's the due diligence solution.

The correct framing: SI8 is what protects you when a regulator asks **how** you knew the synthetic performer was compliant — not just whether you labeled it.

### What to avoid:
- Claiming SI8 is required by the NY law (it isn't — the law requires disclosure, not Chain of Title)
- Using the NY law as a hook without clarifying that SI8 addresses the documentation gap around "actual knowledge," not the disclosure label itself
