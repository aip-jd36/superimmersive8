## The centerpiece finding — liability lands on the BRAND

This is the most important item in this document. It is why brand legal has a direct, self-interested reason to demand documentation from agencies before approval — which is the entire basis of SI8's cascade / demand-generator thesis.

- **Hunton (Jeremy S. Boczko and Andre Earls, Jun 3, 2026):** brands must build "proper limitations, indemnities, and clearance into master services agreements" with agencies/vendors, because "in all likelihood, **the retailer, not the third party, will be subject to an enforcement action.**" → When an agency's AI ad infringes, the *brand* gets sued, not the agency's AI tool. The brand therefore has every incentive to require independent assurance from the agency up front.

## Risk 1 — "Platform access is not legal clearance" (SI8's core distinction, confirmed)

- **Influencers-Time (Mar 2026):** "Marketers should not assume that because an AI platform allowed the prompt, the output is safe to use. Platform access is not legal clearance." Output "may trigger copyright, right of publicity, false endorsement, trademark, or unfair competition claims, especially when used in commerce."
- **Cummings & Cummings (attorney/CPA, Mar 2026):** executives wrongly assume "if the business 'created' content using a tool, the business owns the copyright." The "appearance of automation does not simplify governance; it multiplies the number of decisions that must be made and documented."
- **SI8 relevance:** This is exactly the agency-uses-a-licensed-tool ≠ campaign-is-cleared distinction. An agency having a Runway/Kling/Firefly subscription is not clearance; independent assessment is what closes that gap.

## Risk 2 — Commercial use raises the stakes vs. artistic/personal use

- **Influencers-Time:** "Advertising raises the stakes because the content is commercial. A brand is not creating experimental art for private use. It is using the output to sell products or services, which makes rights holders more likely to challenge it." Courts may view "intentional prompting with a named artist as evidence that the advertiser tried to capture protected expression."
- **SI8 relevance:** The commercial/advertising ICP has *more* acute exposure than the festival-art-film case — the live agency/enterprise segment is the higher-pain one, not the deferred film track.

## Risk 3 — The prompt-authorship trap (again, now in advertising)

- **Ogilvy (marketers' guidance):** "meticulously document your team's creative involvement… as courts are increasingly denying copyright to works generated solely by AI."
- **Cummings:** "the compliance surface area expands with every prompt."
- **Hunton (verified independently — see Source Verification):** copyright law requires human authorship; works generated "without meaningful human involvement generally are not subject to protection." Recommends "maintaining logs of sketches, prompts, and edits" as proof of authorship — and flags that many generative-AI tools treat user prompts as non-confidential, a separate exposure.
- **SI8 relevance (and a mirror on our own methodology):** Prompt-only work has weak authorship. An honest SI8 assessment of a prompt-only submission must reflect that weakness, not stamp it clean — this is the same gap Reviewer Manual v0.2 (Jul 20, 2026) already fixed in Domain H (corroborating artifact required, not just a detailed narrative). This document is now a second, independent, advertising-specific confirmation of that same legal reality, alongside the Thoolie film evidence.

## Risk 4 — Training-data contamination pulls the advertiser into others' disputes

- **Influencers-Time:** "If the AI system was trained on copyrighted works without permission… even if the advertiser did not build the model, it may still be pulled into claims, takedowns, or reputational fallout once a campaign goes live."
- **Hunton:** infringing outputs "can be easily identified by copyright holders through… reverse image and audio search engines."
- **SI8 relevance:** Unquantifiable residual risk — the honest posture is to document and disclose it, not claim to clear it. (Reverse-image detection makes it *findable*, which raises the stakes of shipping it undocumented.)

## Risk 5 — Right of publicity / likeness / false endorsement

- **Hunton (verified independently):** "If an ad involves or resembles an identified person, retailers should consider whether it is appropriate to obtain a release or permission. Failure to do so can lead to legal action and reputational harm."
- **Influencers-Time:** heightened "when the artist is famous, deceased with an active estate, or publicly associated with certain values."
- **SI8 relevance:** Directly maps to SI8 Domain L (Likeness & Performer Rights); the commercial context makes it more acute (named individuals + endorsement implications).

## Risk 6 — Trademark / brand-confusion exposure

- **Varnum (Mar 2026):** generative AI "has lowered the barrier to producing large volumes of potentially infringing or confusingly similar content."
- **Hunton (verified independently):** "AI advertising creators must check for potential trademark infringement in their content by clearing key names, logos, and design elements before materials go live."
- **Trestle Law (Mar 2026):** in disputes, "opposing parties often scrutinize how a mark was selected. Evidence that a brand relied solely on AI recommendations without diligence can weaken claims of good faith and increase exposure to damages."
- **SI8 relevance:** Documented human diligence (which SI8 assesses) is itself protective; its absence increases damages exposure.

## Risk 7 — FTC / deceptive-advertising exposure (UNIQUE to commercials — not in the film evidence)

- **Taft (Apr 2026):** "If AI writes product claims, performance statements, comparisons, or guarantees, someone needs to verify each one. The FTC's rule is direct: ads must be truthful, advertisers must have evidence to back up their claims, and ads cannot be unfair."
- **Influencers-Time:** if an ad implies a human artist created/approved content when they didn't, the brand may face "misleading representation" scrutiny.
- **SI8 relevance:** A regulatory risk category that applies to advertising specifically. Note the boundary: SI8 assesses *rights/provenance evidence*, not claim-substantiation — flag FTC exposure as adjacent, don't claim to clear it.

## What the law firms recommend brands/agencies DO — which maps to SI8's scope

- **Varnum:** require disclosure of AI use; obtain reps & warranties of non-infringement; clarify IP ownership; allocate indemnification for third-party claims.
- **Hunton (verified independently):** "Creating a company-wide procedure that identifies the requisite terms, tools, and permissions that are required before using generative AI for advertising is crucial for long-term success" — plus the master-services-agreement clearance language from the centerpiece finding.
- **Cummings:** "integrate traditional clearance into your AI workflows."
- **Taft:** "clearance should happen before launch, before ad spend, and before public rollout." Maintain "human control, clearance, substantiation, documentation, and launch review."
- **Ogilvy:** "maintain a checklist, document AI prompts and modifications, and seek legal review."

Every one of these — disclosure, non-infringement verification, IP clarification, documented human contribution, pre-launch review — is something SI8's assessment produces or independently verifies. The difference: SI8 provides it as an *independent third-party opinion*, not the agency's own self-attestation.

## STRATEGIC REALITY — read before using in sales

1. **This is the LIVE-ICP evidence base.** Unlike the film/E&O one-pager (deferred ICP-3 research), this validates ICP 1 (Agency) and ICP 2 (Legal) — the segment SI8 is pursuing now. The Hunton "brand gets sued, not the agency" finding is the documented core of the cascade thesis and belongs in the enterprise/brand-legal pitch.
2. **The incumbent solution is "hire a lawyer" — even more so than in film.** Every source is a law firm whose implicit pitch is "retain us for clearance" (Trestle offers exactly this). Ogilvy also points brands to tool-level indemnity (Adobe Firefly). SI8 competes against BOTH clearance counsel AND tool indemnity. SI8's lane is narrower and specific: the *independent assessment between the agency and the brand's legal review* — not a replacement for the brand's counsel, not the tool's indemnity, but the thing the brand can rely on that isn't the agency's own word and isn't a per-campaign attorney engagement. Sell that lane precisely; don't claim "we solve AI legal risk."
3. **Facts vs. framing.** These are IP firms incentivized to emphasize risk. The underlying legal facts (Thaler line, FTC truth-in-advertising rules, right-of-publicity law, the brand-carries-liability principle) are real and verifiable — cite those. Discount the severity theatrics the way you'd want a buyer to skeptically read SI8's own materials.
4. **Copyright hygiene for SI8's own use:** paraphrase, don't lift these firms' phrasing into site copy; reference the underlying facts/case law (not copyrightable), attribute where quoted, keep excerpts short.
