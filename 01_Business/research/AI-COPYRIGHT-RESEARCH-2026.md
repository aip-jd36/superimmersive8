# AI Output Copyright Research — SI8 Implications
**Created:** July 2, 2026
**Status:** Living document — update as case law and guidance evolve

## The Core Question

> Can AI-generated video be copyrighted — and what does the answer mean for SI8?

This is a deceptively simple question with a complex commercial answer. The legal answer and the commercial answer are different, and the gap between them is where SI8's value lives.

---

## Current Legal Landscape

### United States

**The rule:** AI-generated content without human authorship cannot be copyrighted.

**Key sources:**
- **Thaler v. Perlmutter** — US Court of Appeals affirmed: AI-generated works are not copyright protected. Supreme Court declined to hear the case.
- **US Copyright Office 2025 report** — "After considering the extensive public comments and the current state of technological development, our conclusions turn on the centrality of human creativity to copyright. Extending protection to material whose expressive elements are determined by a machine would undermine rather than further the constitutional goals of copyright."
- **Allen v. Perlmutter** — Pending. Creator used 600+ iterative prompts. Will test how much human creative input is required before copyright attaches. Decision expected to set the "human contribution" threshold.

**The critical qualifier:** The word "entirely" matters. US Copyright Office language: "entirely generated by AI cannot be protected." Where a human makes significant creative contributions to an AI-assisted work, those contributions may be copyrightable. The line is not yet settled.

**Practical implication:** Purely prompt-to-output AI video = no copyright. Human-directed, iteratively edited AI video = possibly partial copyright on the human-authored elements.

### European Union

- EU copyright law also requires human authorship for protection.
- No specific AI copyright ruling at EU level yet (as of July 2026).
- Individual member states vary. Austria and Germany: copyright granted to physical/natural persons only.
- EU AI Act (Art. 50) addresses disclosure/labeling of AI content — separate from copyright ownership.

### Other Jurisdictions

- Sweden: no copyright protection for AI output (confirmed per PRV guidance).
- Most common-law jurisdictions: following US/UK direction — human authorship required.
- Not yet globally harmonized.

## The Ownership vs. Clearance Distinction

This is the most important distinction for SI8's business model.

| Question | Who asks it | Answer for pure AI output |
|----------|-------------|--------------------------|
| **Does anyone own copyright in this video?** | Creator, agency | Probably no (in US) |
| **Is it safe to use this commercially?** | Brand legal team | Unresolved — depends on multiple factors |

These are completely different questions. SI8 answers the second one.

**"No one owns the copyright" does NOT mean:**
- The content is safe to deploy commercially
- There are no third-party IP claims
- E&O insurance will cover the campaign
- Competitors cannot copy and reuse it

**"No one owns the copyright" DOES mean:**
- The output is effectively in the public domain
- Anyone can use it (including your competitors)
- No DMCA takedown rights if someone copies it
- No ability to license or monetize the content exclusively

## Risks That Persist Even When No One Owns the Copyright

### 1. Training Data Liability
Even if the output has no copyright, the process of generating it may have infringed on copyrights in the training data. If a court rules that outputs from models trained on unlicensed data are "tainted," commercial use creates exposure.

**Active litigation:** Getty Images v. Stability AI, Authors Guild v. OpenAI, multiple music label suits against AI companies. Unresolved as of July 2026.

### 2. Likeness Rights / Right of Publicity
If the video contains a recognizable person's face, voice, or distinctive persona — right of publicity law applies regardless of copyright status. No copyright in the output does not protect against a likeness claim.

**Relevant law:** NY Synthetic Performer Act (S.8420-A, effective June 9, 2026) — explicitly targets AI-generated synthetic performers. See `01_Business/research/NY-SYNTHETIC-PERFORMER-LAW-2026.md`.

### 3. Music and Audio Rights
Any audio in the video carries separate rights. Music licensing (sync + master) is entirely independent of video copyright status.

### 4. Derivative Works
If the AI output closely resembles an existing copyrighted work — even accidentally — the original work's copyright may still apply. The output being uncopyrightable does not protect against a derivative work claim by the original rights holder.

### 5. Trademark and Brand Elements
Recognizable logos, brand marks, or product designs in frame create trademark exposure independent of copyright.

### 6. The Copycat Risk (Commercial, Not Legal)
Because no one owns the copyright, competitors can legally copy the video and run it in their own campaigns. A $15M media spend on an asset that competitors can freely copy may be commercially unacceptable to a brand — even if legally permitted.

### 7. E&O Insurance Lockout
Errors & Omissions (E&O) insurance underwriters require verified chain of title and documentation of human authorship. A campaign where no one can document ownership and clearance may be uninsurable. Brands launching major paid media without E&O coverage are taking unquantified risk.

## The "Fractional Authorship" Solution

Where agencies incorporate human creative work into AI-generated content, copyright attaches to the human-authored elements:

- Manual editing and compositing
- Color grading decisions
- Licensed music selection and editing
- Human-authored graphics, titles, or overlays
- Directorial choices made during AI generation (iterative prompting with documented creative intent)

SI8's documentation can isolate uncopyrightable AI elements from human-authored elements, establishing "fractional copyright" over the final composite. This:
- Creates a defensible ownership claim on the human-authored portions
- Enables E&O insurance coverage
- Provides documented chain of custody for the creative process

## What This Means for SI8

### The fundamental reframe

Old framing: "Can you copyright AI video?" (binary, legal)

SI8 framing: "Given the evidence, what commercial risks remain, and are they acceptable for this campaign?" (risk assessment, commercial)

**ChatGPT synthesis (July 2, 2026):**
> "Legal uncertainty does not automatically prevent commercial use. It changes the commercial risk profile."

This is the sentence that captures SI8's positioning precisely.

### The commercial risk framework

Copyright status is one input into a commercial risk assessment — not the conclusion.

| Factor | High exclusivity campaign | Short social campaign | Internal use |
|--------|--------------------------|----------------------|--------------|
| No copyright protection | High concern | Low concern | No concern |
| Training data liability | High concern | Medium concern | Low concern |
| Likeness risks | High concern | High concern | Medium concern |
| Copycat risk | High concern | Low concern | No concern |
| E&O insurance | Required | May be required | Not required |

A video with weak copyright protection but clean training data, no likeness issues, and licensed audio may be lower commercial risk than a video with strong human authorship but unclear music rights.

**SI8's output:** Commercial risk assessment across all factors — not a binary copyright determination.

### Sample risk language (from ChatGPT synthesis)

> "Based on current guidance, the extent of copyright protection for this work may be uncertain or limited. This does not prevent commercial deployment but may reduce exclusivity and increase the possibility that substantially similar works could be reused by others. Based on the available evidence, no significant third-party rights issues were identified. Commercial deployment may be appropriate depending on intended use, campaign duration, and the organization's risk tolerance."

This is how lawyers think — not "approved/rejected" but "here are the risks."

### The strongest case study

**Scenario:** AI-generated commercial video. No copyright owner. No IP infringement. All AI tools properly licensed. No likeness issues. Licensed audio.

- Traditional legal answer: uncertain, possibly no protection
- SI8 answer: commercial risk is LOW — absence of copyright reduces exclusivity but does not create third-party liability exposure. Appropriate for marketing campaigns and advertising. Additional consideration recommended for long-term brand characters or franchise assets.

That answer is more useful than "no copyright = don't use it."

## Key Distinctions for Sales Conversations

When a prospect raises the copyright question, reframe:

| What they ask | What they actually need |
|---------------|------------------------|
| "Can we copyright this?" | "Can we safely deploy this commercially?" |
| "Who owns this?" | "Are we exposed to third-party claims?" |
| "Is it protected?" | "Will our E&O insurer cover this?" |
| "What if someone copies it?" | "Is exclusivity important for this campaign?" |
