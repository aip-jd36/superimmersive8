## Hypothesis

Among hands-on commercial AI creators who already do some rights/commercial-use checking (B1's population), does meaningful uncertainty remain **after** they have checked the tools, licenses, source assets, and permissions? If so, does it cause consequential behavior — creative changes, dropping an asset/element, delay, escalation, outside review, client/legal involvement, or avoidance?

## Core research question

> "After creators perform their normal commercial-use checks, does meaningful uncertainty remain — and does it change what they do?"

## What counts as evidence (and what doesn't)

Mirrors B1's discipline (`CR-TRACK-B-CREATOR-READINESS-SPEC.md` §"What counts as evidence"): a stated opinion that "copyright is important" is not evidence of residual decision friction. The evidence B2 needs is behavioral/consequential — a specific instance where a check came back unclear or incomplete, and what happened next.

## Falsification logic

**Positive:** creators describe a specific post-check unresolved case; that uncertainty caused a real production-behavior change (dropped, delayed, escalated, avoided, sought outside input); the pattern recurs across respondents rather than being a one-off.

**Negative:** creators consistently report their own checks resolve the question; "I can normally figure it out myself and it doesn't materially affect the project" (explicitly valid negative evidence per the parent memo, not a failed probe); uncertainty is described but never changes behavior.
