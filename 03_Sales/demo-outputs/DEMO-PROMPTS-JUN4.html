"What did we learn from our split tests?"
Response — Claude Code

Test 2 — EU Act Trigger: Deadline-First vs. Client-Pull

Confirmed Client-pull framing ("your clients are now asking because of August") outperforms deadline-first ("EU AI Act enforcement starts August 2") for UK / financial services leads.

Ivan Petruzzelli (State Street Investment Mgmt, London) replied "Yes we are" within 24 hours of receiving the client-pull message — re-activating a lukewarm lead who had gone cold after his initial reply.

Geo exception: Nordic and German leads appear to bring up the August 2 deadline unprompted — Henrik Sylvén (Stockholm) cited the EU AI Act deadline without prompting. Deadline-first may work better in DE/SE, where regulatory awareness is organic.

Test 3 — Sample Output vs. 9-Question Survey

Working Process-first (9 questions) works for leads with informal, developing workflows. Ibrahim Badi answered 4 of 9 questions inline without prompting — "Creator Record makes sense as next step."

Warning Process-first repels leads who have already formalized their own solution. Kris Tan (Mocreative, SG) explicitly guards his internal workflow. Shyan Pawl and Derek Du Chesne have already baked AI terms into contracts — the 9 questions read as redundant.

Gap identified: A third arm is needed for "already-solved-informally" leads — a differentiation frame: "here's how our output compares to what you're already doing."

Test 1 — Long vs. Short Product Description

Voided Lilly alias inaccessible — the short-form variant (v1-B) had no leads. Cannot conclude. Needs to be relaunched with a different alias when 3+ Bucket 2 profile leads surface.

Most Actionable Learning

Before sending a follow-up, classify the lead's prior reply: do they have no solution (send questions), an informal solution (send questions), or a formalized solution (send differentiation frame). Same product, three different messages.
