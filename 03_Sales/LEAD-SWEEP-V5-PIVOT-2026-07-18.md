# Lead Sweep vs. the v5 Institutional Pivot — 2026-07-18

**Purpose:** The marketing site moved from v4 (Compliance-as-a-Service: $29/$499 tiered, self-serve, "get your video cleared") to v5 (Institutional: "Independent Commercial Assurance for AI Media," no pricing shown, single CTA "Book a call," positioned as an outside opinion legal/procurement can rely on — see `07_Website/index.html`, live since 2026-07-12). This document sweeps every active lead (hot/warm/cold) against that new positioning, asks which ones we think would actually respond to it, and asks whether the ICP 1/2/3 framework in `03_Sales/ICP-DEFINITIONS.md` (last updated 2026-06-28, written entirely against the v4 self-serve product) still holds.

**This is a brainstorming input, not a decision document.** Section D is a self-contained prompt for workshopping this with other AI chat agents — it carries its own context and doesn't depend on repo access.

## C. Is the ICP 1/2/3 Framework Still Valid?

Following the Decision Quality Standards format, since this bears on where outreach effort goes next.

**FACTS:**
- `ICP-DEFINITIONS.md` (2026-06-28) is written entirely against the v4 self-serve $499/$29 product — every buyer-behavior claim in it ("Price point: $499 likely self-authorizable at agency level," "decision speed... self-authorize at $499") assumes a transactional, price-visible motion that no longer exists on the primary site.
- The v5 homepage's core narrative — *"The demand starts with legal. The work lands on the agency. SI8 serves both ends of that handoff"* — is, almost verbatim, the ICP 3 → ICP 1a cascade thesis that `ICP-DEFINITIONS.md` describes as the "highest-leverage target despite the smallest immediate pool." The pivot did not abandon that thesis; it put it on the homepage as the primary story.
- Matthew Sergison-Main's (B088, ICP 1a) reply is now the homepage's lead testimonial, and Ivan Petruzzelli (B130) and Dan Lantry (B158) — both explicitly cited as ICP 3 confirming leads — are also the two strongest Hot/Warm leads in the current sweep.
- v5's copy has no visible reference to E&O insurance, broadcast clearance, or Line Producer/Business Affairs language — the entire ICP 2 mechanism (clearance-gate, E&O-gate) is absent from the primary narrative, even though ICP 2's own confirming leads (Steve Bannerman, Justin Lufair Brown) are real and still in the pipeline.
- There is no trace of a self-serve, no-sales-call path in the v5 primary site — the Creator Record ($29) / SI8 Certified ($499) self-authorize model that most of ICP 1a's own definition depends on for "decision speed" appears to have been removed from the front-line funnel.

**HYPOTHESES (not yet validated):**
- ICP 3 (brand legal / demand generator) and the upper end of ICP 1a (holdco-embedded, enterprise-scale CD) are now *more* central to the model than before — the positioning shift may have simply caught up to where the actual confirming data already pointed, rather than invalidating it. **Validation method:** track close rate and time-to-call specifically for ICP 3 and enterprise ICP 1a leads under the v5 "book a call" motion vs. their v4-era pace.
- The long tail of ICP 1a — individual/small-shop CDs and production people without holdco or enterprise-client backing — may no longer be a viable direct-buy segment if there's truly no self-serve path left. **Validation method:** re-approach 5–10 Cluster 2 cold leads with the v5 framing specifically and see if any convert to a booked call at all; if none do within a normal reply window, that's a real signal the self-serve-dependent segment of ICP 1a has become unreachable through the current funnel.
- ICP 2 (BA/Broadcast/E&O) may have been implicitly deprioritized by the v5 copy rewrite rather than deliberately dropped — this could be an oversight (the two-audience homepage framing is simpler to write and ICP 2 didn't fit cleanly into "agency + brand legal") rather than a strategic decision. **Validation method:** confirm directly whether the v5 rewrite was meant to fold ICP 2 into "the agency" audience, or whether it was dropped without a decision being made either way.
- Steve Bannerman sitting uncontacted-at-Warm-tier for multiple cycles despite being ICP 2's strongest quote may itself be indirect evidence that ICP 2 has quietly lost attention since the pivot began, independent of any explicit decision to deprioritize it.

**OPEN QUESTIONS:**
- Was dropping the self-serve $29/$499 path from the primary site (07_Website/index.html) a deliberate decision, or a side effect of the v5 copy rewrite? This matters because it directly determines whether Cluster 2 (the ~30 individual/freelance cold leads) is still addressable at all, or whether they were implicitly written off when the pivot happened.
- Does "book a call, no price shown" reduce the volume of inbound interest from the individual/freelance segment specifically — and if so, is that an acceptable trade given the higher per-deal value of institutional buyers, or does it just shrink the funnel without a compensating increase in institutional conversion?
- Should ICP 2 (BA/Broadcast/E&O) be explicitly re-confirmed as a target segment under v5, folded into "the agency" audience, or formally deprioritized? Right now it's ambiguous by omission, not by decision.
- Is there a plan to route the individual/self-serve-inclined segment somewhere (e.g., keep the legacy `/record` and `/certify` pages live as a lower-tier path even though they're unlinked from v5 nav), or has that segment effectively been let go?

**CONFIDENCE:** Moderate. The core finding — that v5's two-audience narrative validates rather than contradicts the existing ICP 3 → ICP 1a cascade thesis — is well-supported by both the copy itself and the fact that the two strongest confirming leads are already the two strongest active leads. The weaker parts of this analysis (ICP 2's status, the fate of the self-serve-dependent long tail) are genuinely open questions this document can't resolve with the data on hand — they need a direct decision from JD, not more pipeline analysis.

## D. Portable Brainstorming Prompt (for other AI chat agents)

**Context:** I run SuperImmersive 8 (SI8), a small (solo-founder) B2B compliance/assurance business for AI-generated commercial video, based in Taipei. Until July 12, 2026, our public positioning was a "Compliance-as-a-Service" model: a tiered, self-serve product ($29 self-attested "Creator Record," $499 human-reviewed "SI8 Certified"), sold directly off the website with no sales call required, plus an opt-in creator marketplace on the side.

On July 12, 2026, I pivoted the public site to an institutional model. The new homepage removes all pricing, removes the self-serve signup path, and replaces it with a single CTA: "Book a call." The new positioning: *"SI8 is the independent third party that reviews the evidence [behind an AI-generated commercial video] and issues a commercial assurance opinion both sides can rely on."* The new homepage frames the business around one specific handoff: **"The demand starts with legal. The work lands on the agency. SI8 serves both ends of that handoff."** Two audiences are named explicitly: (1) the agency/production team that made the AI content and needs to prove it's clean, and (2) the brand's legal/procurement team that has to approve it and doesn't want to rely on the agency's own word. The deliverable is now called an "Assessment Report" — a structured PDF with an outcome, findings, residual risks, and recommended next steps, delivered after a human review process (roughly 90 minutes of reviewer time per assessment). No price is disclosed publicly; it's negotiated on the call.

**Before the pivot, I had built a detailed ICP (ideal customer profile) framework from ~600+ LinkedIn cold-outreach replies, organized into three tiers:**

- **ICP 1a — Agency Creative/Production at finserv-exposed clients:** Creative Directors, Production Specialists at mid-size agencies or holdco arms (e.g. Brandtech/OLIVER, IPG, S4 Capital) whose clients include regulated brands (banks, insurers, fintechs). The trigger is the *client's* legal team demanding AI documentation on delivered work. This was assumed to be a fast, self-authorized $499 purchase.
- **ICP 1b — In-house finserv creative:** Same pain, but the person works directly inside a regulated financial company, and their own compliance team is the trigger. Slower buying cycle (internal procurement), and campaigns specifically targeting this segment via LinkedIn's "Financial Services" industry filter performed badly — the filter mostly surfaced financial analysts, not creatives.
- **ICP 2 — Business Affairs/Broadcast Affairs/Line Producer (clearance + E&O insurance):** People who already run a formal clearance process for music/talent/locations and need AI added to that stack, or Line Producers who can't get Errors & Omissions insurance on productions with AI content without documentation. This ICP doesn't appear anywhere in the new v5 homepage copy, even though it has real confirming leads (e.g. a Head of Post-Production at a major streaming studio saying "provenance of the video is HUGELY important").
- **ICP 3 — Brand Legal/Agency General Counsel (the "demand generator"):** People who *create* the requirement that flows down to ICP 1 — brand IP counsel, holdco AI governance leads, agency GCs. The thesis: one brand legal team that pre-approves our documentation format creates cascading demand from every agency on their roster, without us having to sell each agency individually. This is a small pool (single digits of confirmed leads) but treated as the highest-leverage segment because of the multiplier effect.

**What I've noticed since the pivot:** The new homepage's core story ("demand starts with legal, work lands on the agency") is almost word-for-word the ICP 3 → ICP 1a cascade I'd already hypothesized before the pivot — and one of my strongest ICP 1a leads' own quote ("Yes, I'm being asked this — 100%") is now literally the testimonial on the new homepage. So the pivot seems to validate that specific thesis. But I also have a large number of individual freelancers and small-shop creatives in my pipeline who engaged with the *old* cheap, fast, no-sales-call pitch — asking things like "what does it cost" or "can I see an example" — and I'm not sure a "book a call, no price shown, independent assurance opinion" pitch will land with them at all. I may have just made a large chunk of my existing warm/cold leads unconvertible by removing the low-commitment path they were responding to, without necessarily replacing it with anything for them specifically.

**What I want help thinking through:**
1. Does the shift from "cheap self-serve product" to "institutional no-price assurance opinion" logically strengthen the ICP 3 → ICP 1a(enterprise) cascade thesis, or am I pattern-matching too eagerly because one convenient quote happened to fit?
2. Is ICP 2 (Business Affairs / Broadcast / E&O insurance) still a coherent target segment under an institutional-assurance model, or does the two-audience (agency + brand legal) framing actually describe a different, narrower business than the one ICP 2 was built for? Should it be explicitly folded in, explicitly dropped, or run as a separate track?
3. What should happen to the individual-freelancer/small-shop segment of my pipeline that responded well to the old cheap self-serve pitch? Is there a version of "institutional assurance" that still works for them, or is the honest answer that this pivot deliberately (or accidentally) priced them out, and I should stop spending outreach effort on that segment?
4. Is there a risk that removing visible pricing entirely — even from a page whose whole point is to build trust with skeptical legal/procurement buyers — actually reduces credibility rather than increasing it, since sophisticated institutional buyers are often *more* comfortable with a firm that's transparent about cost structure, not less?
5. Any blind spots in how I'm reading my own lead data — am I over-indexing on a small number of loud, well-fitting signals (Ivan Petruzzelli, Matthew Sergison-Main, Dan Lantry, Simon Helm) and ignoring a much larger, quieter population that the new positioning simply doesn't serve?

I'd like a genuinely critical read, not a validation of my own instinct — tell me where this reasoning is thin.
