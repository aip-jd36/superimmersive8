### TEST B — Title Cluster: Creative Director vs. Business Affairs/Ops

**Core question:** Do Business Affairs, Broadcast Affairs, EP, and clearance-ops titles feel AI documentation as an operational problem today (alongside music and talent) while Creative Directors feel it as a future concept?

**Hypothesis:** BA/clearance-ops titles already own a documentation workflow for music, talent, and IP. AI documentation is a natural extension of their existing job. They engage with SI8 as a supplier evaluation, not a concept pitch. CD titles engage with the concept but don't own the clearance process — their pain is downstream.

### TEST D — Pre vs. Post Production Timing

**Core question:** Does framing SI8 as a pre-production compliance tool (documentation built as you create) vs. a post-production verification document (Chain of Title issued after delivery) produce different reply and conversion patterns?

**Hypothesis:** SI8's current product is framed as a post-delivery document. Tim Deussen's critique — "currently still disconnected from the technical process" — suggests that for compliance-aware buyers, the more acute pain is at production kickoff: embedding documentation into the workflow, not issuing a certificate after delivery. If beachhead buyers need pre-production compliance, SI8 may need to extend upstream.

**This is also a product-definition test:** If v-D1 (pre-production) lands significantly better than v-D2 (post-production), it signals SI8 may need to add a pre-production offering — intake forms, workflow checklists, real-time documentation tools — not just a final certificate.

**What we're measuring:**
- Does "document as you create" framing get more replies than "verification document after delivery"?
- Do replies to v-D1 describe a real need for production-stage documentation, or do they reframe back to post-delivery?
- Does v-D2 (current framing) produce more concrete product questions (price, format, turnaround)?

**Variant D1 — Pre-production gate framing:**

### TEST E — Current Solution Probe ("What Are You Using Now?")

**Core question:** Among leads who describe informal documentation processes (spreadsheets, email trails, metadata notes, contract terms), how much time are they spending — and does surfacing the format gap convert them faster than a product pitch?

**Hypothesis:** Leads who have already built an informal workaround have self-validated the problem. They are further along than "nothing yet" leads. But their informal solution has a hidden gap: it doesn't produce a document a brand legal team can actually reference. Probing the time cost + format gap will surface self-qualified buyers faster than a product description.

**Source of insight:** Test 1 non-responder audit (Jun 11) — 6 of 9 v1-A non-responders described informal processes in their original reply. They didn't respond to 9 survey questions because they believed their current approach was adequate. They needed a differentiation argument, not a question list.

**What we're measuring:**
- Does the current-solution + time-cost probe produce more specific replies than the 9-question survey?
- Does the format gap framing ("your notes aren't the problem — the format is") convert informal-solution leads faster than product-first messaging?
- Are informal-solution leads more valuable than no-solution leads, or more entrenched?

**Probe message template (v-E1):**
> "Most people I speak to who track this say they're doing it informally — spreadsheets, email trails, that sort of thing. Out of curiosity: how long does it take you per project? I ask because the gap usually shows up when a client's legal team asks for it in a specific format, and the informal version doesn't satisfy that."
