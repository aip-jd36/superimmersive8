## Why this restructuring happened

The v5 site pivot (2026-07-12) moved SI8 from a self-serve, price-visible, tiered CaaS product to an institutional model: no pricing shown, single "Book a call" CTA, positioned as an independent third-party opinion. On 2026-07-18–19, a full lead sweep against that new positioning (`LEAD-SWEEP-V5-PIVOT-2026-07-18.md`) plus a three-way AI strategy review produced these decisions:

1. **The freelancer/individual-creator self-serve long tail is now formally out of scope** as an active pipeline segment — not a target of outreach effort, tracked, or chased. It survives only as an unpublished, unmarketed legacy fallback (see `01_Business/pricing/PRICING-STRATEGY-v3.0.md`).
2. **ICP 2 (Legal) and enterprise-scale ICP 1 (Agency) are now the center of the model**, not a validation of a side hypothesis — the v5 homepage's core narrative ("the demand starts with legal, the work lands on the agency") is, close to verbatim, the cascade thesis this doc already described before the pivot. **Caveat (ChatGPT's synthesis point, worth repeating everywhere this comes up): this is message validation, not market validation.** No external deal has closed under v5 yet. Every buyer-behavior claim below should be read as a working hypothesis until a real sales call or real assessment confirms it.
3. **ICP 3 (Line Producer/EP — Film) is deferred**, not abandoned. Reasoning below.

## ICP 1: Agency (Direct Buyer — Single Assessment)

**The mechanism:** A brand's legal team adds a documentation requirement to the agency brief or contract — the same way they'd add a brand-safety requirement — because they don't trust the agency's own word that AI-generated content is clean. The agency's Creative Director or Production Specialist becomes the person who has to produce that proof for every AI campaign they deliver. SI8 sells them a single Commercial Assurance Assessment: one video (or campaign asset) in, one Assessment Report out.

**Engagement model post-pivot:** Transactional, single-asset, ad hoc-priced. **$499 is a floor hypothesis, not a fixed price** — test upward on high-stakes engagements (a campaign with real media spend blocked behind legal approval can likely absorb more than $499; two prior market signals calling $499 "a good price" may mean it's underpriced, not confirmed-correct). JD sets price per call using judgment, with time-limited discount codes as the negotiation lever — there is no formal randomized price test running. Price is not shown on any nav-linked page; a pricing page exists but is unlisted, shared by direct link only when a prospect asks before booking a call. The assessment methodology and rigor are identical regardless of price paid — SI8 never issues a "lighter" opinion for less money.

## ICP 2: Legal (Demand Generator / Governance Buyer) — formerly "ICP 3"

**The mechanism:** Brand IP/Legal Counsel, Agency General Counsel, and Holdco AI Governance leads are the people who *create* the requirement that flows down to ICP 1. They sit one level above the agency CD and write the contract language or internal policy that makes Chain-of-Title-shaped documentation mandatory. If one brand legal team pre-approves SI8's format and requires it from their agencies, it cascades to every agency on their roster without SI8 selling each one individually.

**The sharper framing (added 2026-07-19, from the ChatGPT strategy review):** An agency asks *"can you assess this campaign?"* Legal asks *"what do we require every agency to do?"* That second question is **policy, not a single transaction** — Policy → Agencies → Assessments → Governance. ICP 2 buyers aren't buying a bigger report; they're buying a standard they can impose downstream. This is why ICP 2 is the Enterprise-motion buyer, structurally, not just a bigger-budget version of ICP 1.

```
Title:        Brand IP/Legal Counsel, Agency General Counsel,
              Holdco AI Governance, EVP Business & Legal Affairs,
              Head of Legal & Business Affairs, independent AI compliance advisor
Company:      Major brands (financial services, pharma, luxury),
              holdco legal/compliance teams, streaming platforms
Geo:          UK, USA, Singapore
Trigger:      Internal AI governance policy or legal review of AI content
              risk — they're building requirements, not responding to them
Pain:         No standardized format exists for what they should require
              from agencies
Buyer:        Legal/compliance, not creative — different budget and process
              from ICP 1
Engagement:   Enterprise motion — NOT "the agency assessment plus features."
              A different engagement built on the identical assessment
              methodology (this distinction protects SI8's independence —
              never sell a "more rigorous" opinion for more money, only a
              different level of procurement/governance support)
Price:        Custom-scoped, multiples above ICP 1 — justified by the
              engagement (MSA/DPA, PO/vendor onboarding, named reviewer,
              executive briefings, consolidated view across a brand's
              agency roster), not by a "better" opinion
Sales motion: Research frame, not pitch. "We're getting evaluated by brand
              legal teams — does our format match what you'd require from
              agencies you commission?"
```

**⚠️ Delivery guardrail (critical, added 2026-07-19):** Do not sell SLA-bound turnaround (24–48hr), a live multi-agency dashboard, or retainer-based bulk allocations as *currently available* — a solo founder cannot honor those yet, and breaking that promise to the strongest cascade lead in the pipeline is worse than not landing the deal. **Sell the vision; scope actual v1 delivery as a defined pilot** ("we'll assess your next N agency submissions against a defined standard and give you a consolidated view"). Build the full SLA/dashboard/retainer program only once it can genuinely be staffed.

## ICP 3: Line Producer / EP (Film) — formerly the E&O/clearance half of "ICP 2" — **DEFERRED, HOLD ALL OUTREACH**

**Status as of 2026-07-19: fully shelved. No active or planned outreach. Do not contact existing leads in this segment. Revisit only if market feedback changes — no revisit date set.**

**The mechanism (why this was ever considered its own ICP):** Line Producers and Executive Producers in film/TV need E&O (Errors & Omissions) insurance, and insurers are starting to add AI exclusions or require documentation before issuing full coverage. Business Affairs/Broadcast Affairs titles at production companies already run a formal clearance process for music, talent, and locations — AI is a new, undocumented category in a workflow they already own.

**Why it's deferred — three compounding reasons (synthesized from the 2026-07-19 Opus/ChatGPT/Gemini review, all three independently reached the same conclusion):**

1. **Wrong channel.** SI8's entire validated outreach engine is LinkedIn cold outreach. Line Producers and EPs are structurally hard to reach that way — the segment requires a different channel entirely (IMDbPro, guild networks, film-commission lists, warm referral). Pursuing it means building a new acquisition motion from scratch, not redirecting existing campaigns.
2. **Wrong product shape.** SI8's current assessment framework (CertForm, the 7-domain reviewer workbook, "independent viewing of the content before reading declarations") assumes the deliverable *is* the AI content — one finished asset, one assessment. Feature film/TV production is typically mostly live-action with AI used for isolated elements (background replacement, VFX, plate cleanup, de-aging). The actual assessment object is different: *identify which elements are AI-touched within a much larger conventional production, and assess whether any of them introduces an unindemnifiable gap in the chain of title* — not "watch the finished film and assess it." That's a different evidence chain (multiple vendors → live action → editorial → VFX → AI components → final film, not Runway project → finished commercial → assessment), and it likely requires a frame-level or shot-level review process SI8 doesn't currently have.
3. **Wrong buyer ecosystem.** Line Producers, Production Counsel, Studio Legal, VFX Supervisors, completion-bond providers, and E&O insurers are a different world from Creative Directors, agency legal, and brand legal — different sales motion, different relationships, different sales cycle.

**Net effect:** ICP 3 isn't "the same business, lower priority." It's a second business to build later — new channel, new product shape, new buyer, at minimum.

## What Is Not an ICP (Common False Positives)

- **Disclosure buyers:** Say "yes" to the gate question but mean "I tell clients which tools I used." Self-exclude when shown a sample.
- **EU Act labeling leads:** Cite the EU AI Act but describe a labeling/disclosure need, not IP clearance. Confirmed false positive pattern in France/Paris campaign data — 6 signals, 0 Chain-of-Title descriptions.
- **T3 builders:** Already building their own internal documentation process. Require a differentiation frame, not a "you need this" pitch.
- **Boutique founders (general), off-geo production, financial advisors/analysts (LinkedIn "AD"/"Associate" titles):** Same false-positive patterns as before — unchanged by the pivot.
- **Individual freelancers/small-shop creatives responding to "what does it cost" curiosity:** New addition (2026-07-19). Not a false positive in the sense of wrong-fit — many of these leads genuinely had real, informal-process pain. But they are **out of scope by decision**, not by ICP mismatch: the institutional, no-price, book-a-call motion is a poor fit for how they were engaging, and the self-serve path that used to serve them is now unpublished and unmarketed. See `LEAD-SWEEP-V5-PIVOT-2026-07-18.md` Cluster 2 for the full list.

## Regulatory Framing — What SI8 Does and Doesn't Do

**Use this section for call prep and to prevent messaging drift in campaigns.** Unchanged by the pricing/ICP restructuring — still accurate.

### EU AI Act Art. 50

| Sub-article | Who it applies to | What it requires | SI8 position |
|-------------|------------------|-----------------|-------------------|
| Art. 50(2) — machine-readable marking | **Providers** (Runway, Kling, Veo) | C2PA or equivalent embedded at generation | SI8 restores what gets stripped in post-production assembly — the statutory obligation is on the provider, not the agency |
| Art. 50(4) — visible disclosure | **Deployers** (agencies, brands) | Human-recognisable labelling of AI content | SI8's review supports this but does not itself constitute the disclosure |

**What SI8 does NOT do:** Guarantee Art. 50 compliance. **Do not pitch as:** "Use SI8 to comply with the EU AI Act."

### NY Synthetic Performer Law (S.8420-A, effective Jun 9, 2026)

Requires conspicuous disclosure when AI creates a synthetic replica of an identifiable person in an advertisement. SI8's clearance review checks for synthetic performers and documents consent/rights — directly relevant, but **the agency still has to add the disclosure itself.** Do not pitch SI8 as satisfying the law on its own.

### The correct regulatory hook for outbound

> *"Your client's legal team is asking for proof the content is commercially safe — not just that it's labeled as AI. The regulations are why they started asking. SI8 is the answer to what they're asking for."*

Accurate across EU Act, NY Law, and ASA/CAP Code contexts. Does not overclaim statutory compliance.
